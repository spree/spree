# B2B Storefront Purchasing — company self-service and checkout

**Status:** Implemented, in review (2026-08-27) — backend in spree/spree#14498,
storefront in spree/storefront#214, both open. Everything in Design Details
ships except the node rename, noted below.

**Depends on:** `6.0-b2b-companies-and-catalogs.md` (implemented 2026-08-25 — the
tree, memberships, invitations, the polymorphic address owner, and the Store
API self-service surface this plan puts a face on).

## Summary

The Store API already let a company's members manage their organization —
memberships, invitations, the address book, the subtree's orders — but the
storefront had no UI for any of it, and checkout knew nothing about
companies: the buyer saw only their personal address book, and the
invitation email linked to an acceptance page that did not exist.

This plan adds the missing storefront surface (a company section in the
standard account area, the invitation acceptance page) and makes checkout
company-aware (buying-for-company context, the company address book at the
address step). It deliberately does NOT change how carts and orders are
owned: a purchase keeps its two buyer-side parties — the person who placed
it and the organization it is for — which is both what shipped and what the
major B2B platforms converge on.

The storefront lives in its own repo (`spree/storefront`, branch `6-0-dev`,
cloned into a worktree by `pnpm wt:setup` and run with `pnpm wt:storefront`);
this plan is the single record for the work in both repos.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **No polymorphic owner on Cart/Order.** The address move to
  `owner_type`/`owner_id` worked because an address has exactly one owner. A
  B2B purchase has two buyer-side parties with different jobs: the *person*
  (authentication, "my orders", transactional email, and the party Enterprise
  approvals and spend limits act on) and the *organization* (tax anchor,
  catalog and price resolution, the company orders view). Collapsing them
  into one polymorphic column would make "Alice bought this for Acme"
  unrepresentable. The industry pattern is the same dual axis: the person
  stays on the order and the purchasing organization is attached beside them
  (Shopify's purchasing-entity shape; the B2B builds on Medusa and
  BigCommerce likewise keep the employee and link the company). Spree already
  ships exactly this — `customer_id` plus `company_id`, standing-validated,
  sole-membership resolution, frozen at completion — so the model needs no
  change; this plan is surface, not schema.
- **OSS is the machinery, the buyer portal is Enterprise.** The open-source
  storefront weaves company self-service into the standard account area,
  plain and unstyled beyond the storefront's own design system: members,
  invitations, addresses, company orders. The polished buyer portal —
  approvals inbox, spending dashboards, invoices, roles — is the packaged
  Enterprise product built on this machinery (tier boundary recorded in the
  Enterprise plans repo). Nothing in the OSS storefront may grow
  role-awareness: every member sees every company page, enforced by the
  server's standing checks, and Enterprise narrows via its access-policy
  subclass, not via UI flags.
- **Checkout references the company book; completion still copies.** The
  storefront submits `shipping_address_id` when a saved address is picked,
  and the cart's ownership-guarded writers accepted only customer-owned rows.
  They now also accept an address owned by the cart's
  resolved company **or an ancestor of it** — the same self-and-ancestors
  chain `Company#default_billing_address` walks, so the book a division
  inherits for prefill is the book its buyers can pick from. Standing is
  already enforced upstream (a cart cannot name a company its customer lacks
  standing over), and completion keeps copying the address onto the order,
  so the book row is never frozen by a sale. A company address never enters
  the *customer's* book and vice versa; the guard refuses ids across that
  line exactly as it refuses another customer's.
- **A purchase deletes only the rows it owns.** Pointing a cart at a book
  entry means the cart borrows it, so the bill/ship cascade was narrowed from
  `dependent: :destroy` to the ownerless rows — the guest's typed-in address
  and the snapshot completion writes. Without that, deleting a cart deleted
  whatever it happened to be pointing at, which for a company site is shared
  tenant data any member could have wiped. `Purchase::Addresses#owns_address?`
  is the one predicate; anything else that destroys a purchase asks it rather
  than restating the rule.
- **Company context is explicit past one membership.** A buyer with a single
  standing gets it resolved silently (`sole_standing_company`, shipped); a
  buyer with several picks the node at checkout, writing the cart's
  `company_id` (shipped, standing-validated). The storefront never guesses
  between memberships. Buying personally remains possible: clearing the
  company clears catalog, pricing and tax anchoring with it.
- **The invitation acceptance page is part of this plan and is not
  optional.** `Spree::CompanyMailer` links
  `<storefront_url>/account/company-invitation?token=…`, and that page used
  to 404. It handles both audiences the token flow serves: a new person
  registering (the account is created with the invited email) and an
  existing customer signing in to accept — refusing, as the API does, when
  the signed-in account's email is not the invited one.

## Design Details

### Storefront — account area (`spree/storefront`)

New pages under the authenticated account shell, listed in the account
navigation only when `GET /store/account/companies` returns memberships:

- **`/account/companies`** — the buyer's memberships with ancestor paths;
  links to each node. Skipped (redirect to the node) when there is exactly
  one.
- **`/account/companies/[id]`** — the node page: the name and its ancestor
  path, members with add-by-email (`POST …/members`, which the server turns
  into a membership or an invitation), pending invitations with revoke, and
  the address book (list, create, edit, delete, set-default — all shipped
  endpoints). The name renders read-only: the API allows a rename and
  `updateCompany` is wired in the data layer, but no edit control ships yet —
  see Open Questions. Address rows reuse the storefront's existing
  address-card conventions; defaults are badges, actions behind a menu, as
  the dashboard settled.
- **`/account/companies/[id]/orders`** — the subtree's completed orders
  (`GET …/orders`), rendered with the existing order-list components beside
  the buyer's personal "my orders".
- **`/account/company-invitation`** — the acceptance page (unauthenticated
  route): token lookup shows who invited them to what
  (`GET /store/company_invitations/:token` returns company and store names),
  then either the register form (invited email fixed) or sign-in-and-accept.
  Success lands on the company page.

### Storefront — checkout

- **Company context.** When the authenticated buyer has standing, the cart
  page **and** the checkout header show "Buying for {company}" — a picker
  when several memberships, static text for one, absent for none. Picking
  writes `company_id` on the cart (shipped API); "buy personally" clears it.
  It sits on the cart as well as checkout because pricing context reads the
  cart's company: set later, the buyer reviews a basket priced for the wrong
  audience and watches the totals move at the last step.
- **Address step.** With a company on the cart, `AddressSelector` lists the
  company book **and** the buyer's own, as labelled groups — the company's
  sites first, under the name of the node that owns them, then "Your
  addresses" — with the company's default shipping site preselected. The
  whole list comes from `GET /store/carts/:id/addresses` in one call, grouped
  by the `owner` each entry carries. Company entries submit by id like
  personal ones do; the "new address" form is unchanged and writes a plain
  cart address, never into the company book — curating the book is the
  account area's job. The Edit control is withheld from the company groups:
  those rows belong to the organization, and the customer address endpoint
  would refuse the write.

  **Corrected 2026-10-19.** This first shipped reassembling the list in the
  storefront from `/account/companies`, `/companies/:id` and the two address
  endpoints — three round-trips, and `sole_standing_company` restated in
  TypeScript, which is exactly what the cart endpoint below exists to
  prevent. Asking the cart also fixed a quieter wrong: every company address
  was labelled with the cart's node, so a headquarters dock a division
  inherits was filed under the division. Each entry now names its own owner.
- **Order display.** The order-placed page and the account order page name
  the company the purchase was for (already on the store order serializer).

### Backend deltas (`spree/api`, `spree/core`)

Planned as "widen one guard", and that part held — no migrations, no
serializer changes, since cart and order already report the company and
company addresses already carry `label` and the default flags. What the
build added beyond it is recorded here because the plan under-read how far
the customer-shaped assumptions reached.

- `Spree::Purchase::Addresses#ship_address_id=` / `#bill_address_id=` share
  one `selectable_address` predicate that accepts the buyer's own book or the
  book of the company the purchase is for **and its ancestors**. Everything
  else about the writers is unchanged, including silently nulling on a
  refused id.
- `Company#address_book` names that inherited reading list, and the store
  listing scope uses it. Reads inherit, writes stay with the node that owns
  the row — the Store API authorizes purely by what `scope` returns, so a
  division member must not reach its parent's entries through it.
- **`Spree::HasAddressBook`** (new): owners declare where their default slots
  live (`bill_address_id` for a customer, `default_bill_address_id` for a
  company node), and every caller asks the owner instead of branching on its
  class. `Addresses::Create` / `Update` then work for any owner — before
  this, running a company entry through `Update` returned success while the
  default flag was silently dropped, because every branch that would have set
  it asked for the customer behind the address.
- Each default flag carries three states, not two: `true` claims the slot,
  `false` gives it up when this address is what holds it, and silence leaves
  it alone. The release is a conditional `UPDATE` rather than a read-then-
  write, so a promotion landing in between is not undone.
- Both company address controllers write through the address services and
  share one `CompanyAddressWrites` concern, matching the customer surface.
- Specs: the company-book id accepted on a cart with standing; refused
  without a company, across companies the buyer has no standing over, for a
  descendant node, and for another customer's rows. The three-state flag rule
  is proved against both a customer and a company node in the
  `HasAddressBook` spec, including the race.

### Follow-up (2026-10-15) — the guard was widened, the paths to it were not

The widened guard was only ever reachable from the model. Everything that
resolves a client-supplied address id sat in front of it and narrowed to the
customer first, so a company address was dropped before `selectable_address`
saw it. This round closes that and extends the same reach to the surfaces
staff use, since most B2B orders are keyed in rather than checked out.

- `Spree::Carts::Update#resolve_address_id` looked the id up in
  `cart.customer.addresses`. **This is the bug the storefront work was
  written against**: a buyer picking a company site saw checkout keep whatever
  was in the slot before, with no error. It now asks the cart, which is the
  one place that knows both books.
- `selectable_address` is public and takes a prefixed id or a raw key, so
  every caller shares one rule instead of restating it. `selectable_addresses`
  is its listing counterpart — the buyer's book unioned with the resolved
  company's `address_book`, soft-deleted rows excluded.
- **`GET /store/carts/:id/addresses`** (new, read-only) returns that union.
  Without it a storefront has to reassemble the list from the memberships, the
  company and the personal book, and reimplement `sole_standing_company` to
  know which company an unstated purchase is for — three places to drift from
  the server. Curating either book stays with the account and company
  surfaces; this endpoint only answers "what may this cart use".
  **Signed-in customers only**, unlike every other cart endpoint: the answer
  is the account's two books, not the cart's own contents, and a guest token
  proves possession of a cart — possibly one the buyer adopted from whoever
  started it — rather than of the account. It costs nothing, since a guest
  cart has neither book.
  Each entry carries its `owner` (`SelectableAddressSerializer`, which is the
  address serializer plus that one association) so a picker can label the
  books from the one response. Without it the client can tell a warehouse
  from a flat only by asking which company the cart is for — the resolution
  this endpoint exists to keep on the server. Only this listing needs the
  owner: everywhere else an address appears the book is known from the
  endpoint, and a purchase's own snapshot belongs to no book at all.
- `Spree::Orders::Create` accepts a company site as
  `shipping_address_id`/`billing_address_id`, and `use_customer_default_address`
  fills from the company's defaults (inherited from ancestors) when the request
  **names** one. It does **not** fall back to the buyer's personal default
  there: an order for the organization ships to the organization's sites, and
  substituting someone's home address is exactly the mis-delivery this surface
  exists to prevent.
- `Admin::Companies::AddressesController#index` now lists inherited sites,
  matching the Store controller; writes still only reach the owning node.
- `Address#snapshot` freezes the company line before dropping the owner. A
  node's entry normally stores none and reads the node's name through the
  owner, so every completed B2B order was recording a street with no business
  on it. The copy also keeps its `owner_type` so it still answers
  `business_owned?` — a business address names no person, and without it the
  snapshot starts demanding a contact name it never had.
- **Ownership is `owner_id`, never `owner_type`.** The line above overloads
  the type column: a business snapshot has a type and no id. Every test of
  "does anyone own this row" therefore reads the id — `owns_address?`, and
  `Customers::Anonymize`, which matched on both and so quietly stopped
  erasing the contact details on B2B order snapshots. Nothing fails loudly
  when a privacy sweep covers less than it did, so new code near this column
  gets a spec rather than a careful reading.
- Dashboard: the new-order page offers both books behind the existing
  "use default addresses" switch, through a headless `AddressSelect` in
  `dashboard-ui`.

### Explicitly out of scope

- Roles, approvals, spend limits, order-on-behalf, invoices — Enterprise
  (buyer portal).
- Guest checkout for a company; a company must be bought for by a signed-in
  member.
- Terms/net-payment and quotes — future plans on the Enterprise side.
- The `(wholesale)` demo route group in the storefront stays what it is — a
  customer-group gated catalog demo. The company flow composes with it (a
  wholesale buyer may also be a company member) but shares no code paths.

## Migration Path

Nothing to migrate: no schema changes, no renames. The storefront work is
additive pages in a separate repo; the backend delta widens an acceptance
guard.

## Constraints on Current Work

- New storefront company UI calls only the shipped Store API self-service
  endpoints — no new bespoke endpoints without updating this plan.
- Anything resolving a client-supplied address id for a cart or an order asks
  the purchase (`selectable_address` / `selectable_addresses`), never a book
  directly. Narrowing to the customer in front of the guard is what broke this
  once already, and it fails silently.
- Nothing storefront-side may branch on a member's "role" — OSS has none;
  gate on standing (the server already does) and nothing else.
- Cart/order code must keep treating `customer_id` and `company_id` as two
  independent axes; nothing may infer one from the other beyond the shipped
  `sole_standing_company` fallback.

## Open Questions

Both first-cut questions were answered 2026-08-26 and folded into Design
Details: the address step hid the personal book once a company was on the
cart (strict B2B, the Shopify behaviour), and the company picker appears on
the cart page as well as checkout so catalog pricing is honest while the
buyer is still filling the basket.

The first of those was **reversed on 2026-10-19**: checkout now shows both
books as labelled groups. The strict reading only ever held in the
storefront — the cart has always accepted an id from either book — so hiding
half the list made the UI disagree with the API without preventing anything.
Legibility replaced omission. If the strict behaviour is wanted back, it has
to be enforced on the server (refuse a personal address id once a company is
on the cart), which is a contract change needing its own entry here.

1. **Should a member be able to rename their own company node from the
   storefront?** The Store API allows it and `updateCompany` is wired in the
   storefront data layer, but no edit control ships — the node page renders
   the name read-only. OSS has no company roles, so shipping it means *any*
   member can rename the organization every other member buys under, which
   is a larger blast radius than the rest of the surface (adding a member is
   additive; renaming is not). Left out deliberately rather than forgotten:
   the merchant can rename from the dashboard, and Enterprise's roles decide
   who may in the portal.

2. **Should the cart token rotate when a guest cart is associated to an
   account?** It does not today, so whoever started a cart keeps a working
   token after the buyer signs in and adopts it. The address listing no
   longer accepts that token, but everything else on the cart still does —
   the line items, the chosen addresses, the email. Rotating closes it and
   costs a contract change: the token is on the cart serializer, so the new
   value comes back in the `associate` response, and any client holding the
   old one (a second tab, a retried request) loses the cart. Raised by the
   2026-10-19 review; not decided.
