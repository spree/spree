# B2B Company Self-Registration & Activation Mechanisms

**Status:** In development — design finalized 2026-08-30, implementation started 2026-08-31
**Target:** Spree 6.0 (retargeted 2026-08-30, sequencing review — originally 6.1 on the additive test, pulled forward because the Enterprise onboarding product must ship on 6.0 GA and these mechanisms are its only missing input; every piece remains additive and small)
**Depends on:** B2B Companies & Catalogs (6.0-b2b-companies-and-catalogs.md), B2B Quantity Rules (6.0-b2b-quantity-rules.md — catalog terms), gated storefront access (shipped 5.6)
**Author:** Damian + Claude
**Last updated:** 2026-08-31

## Summary

The B2B flow has no front door: there is no storefront way for a business to
register itself (`/store/companies` is show + update only), and no way for a
merchant to gate pricing on having a commercial relationship. This plan adds
the **open-source mechanisms**: storefront company self-registration, an
`approval_required` storefront access posture ("prices and checkout require
an active company standing"), a machine-readable `pricing_access` reason
code beside hidden prices, and a registered **company activation policy**
consulted everywhere a company's commercial standing matters. In OSS the
default policy activates every company, so registration is instant and the
posture means "company members only" — a useful trade-store feature on its
own. The merchant-approval *flow* (pending applications, verification
requirements, review queue, request-more-information) is the Enterprise
onboarding product built on these mechanisms (tier split decided 2026-08-30
— see `spree-enterprise-v2/docs/plans/b2b-company-onboarding.md`; no
competitor ships approval in open source, and everyone who has it charges
for it).

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **Registration is OSS and companies are born active** (2026-08-30, tier
  split): `POST /api/v3/store/companies` lets an authenticated customer —
  including an existing retail customer — create a root company (kind
  `company`) plus their own membership in one call; a guest storefront
  chains `POST /store/customers` first so the UX is one flow. Free-form
  registration answers land in `metadata['registration']`. Rate-limited
  like customer registration. Core publishes `company.registered` and sends
  no mail.
- **`Spree::Companies::ActivationPolicy` is the single seam** (2026-08-30):
  a dependency-registered class
  (`Spree::Dependencies.company_activation_policy_class`, mirroring
  `storefront_access_policy_class`) answering `active?(company)` and
  `pricing_access_code(user:, store:)`. The OSS default answers active for
  every company. Every place that resolves a company for **visibility,
  pricing, or checkout** consults it: `Catalog.for_company`, both
  `sole_standing_company` implementations (`Purchase::Company` and
  `Products::ForContext` — mirrored, by existing constraint), the cart
  `company_id` write validation, and the `approval_required` posture.
  Subtree semantics (a suspended parent covering its divisions) are the
  policy implementation's job, which is why the seam takes the node.
- **No status column on `spree_companies` in OSS.** The lifecycle
  (pending / review / approved / rejected / suspended, the requirements
  checklist, the queue) lives on an Enterprise-owned application record
  referencing the company — OSS tables never reference Enterprise models,
  and OSS behavior flows entirely through the policy. This supersedes the
  same-day draft that put five statuses on Company.
- **A fourth storefront access posture: `approval_required`** (2026-08-30):
  beside `public | prices_hidden | login_required`. Guests and
  authenticated buyers without a standing over a policy-active company
  browse the catalog with prices null and checkout refused; `prices_hidden`
  keeps its shipped guest-only semantics untouched. Under the default
  policy this reads as "prices for company members only"; under the
  Enterprise policy it becomes "prices for approved companies only".
- **A reason code travels with hidden prices.** Serializers today null
  prices with no explanation. The gating concern computes `pricing_access`
  — `null` (visible) | `login_required` | `company_required` | codes the
  registered policy supplies (Enterprise adds `approval_pending`) —
  surfaced wherever prices are nulled, following the checkout-requirements
  `code:` convention; the channel serializer also exposes
  `storefront_access`. Storefronts render "Awaiting approval" / "Register
  your business" from the code, never by interpreting bare nulls.
- **Checkout is gated by a requirement, standing is not gated at all.** A
  new checkout requirement (`company_activation_required` code) refuses
  customer-initiated completion on `approval_required` channels without a
  policy-active cart company — same shape as `guest_checkout_not_allowed`.
  Self-service standing (`standing_for?`, `company_standing`,
  `Storefront::AccessPolicy`) never consults the policy: members of an
  inactive company keep their company pages, address book, members and
  invitations — that is the pre-approval workspace the Enterprise flow
  needs.
- **Customer-group membership is never an activation mechanism** — groups
  price (catalog assignments), the policy activates. The seeded Wholesale
  group's "approval primitive" comment is retired with this plan.
- **The one-root guard refuses a second founding outright** (2026-08-31,
  implementation): `Companies::Register` rejects with `already_registered`
  when the customer already holds a membership on any root company in the
  store — not just a same-name repeat. A buyer who genuinely operates a
  second business asks staff, who create it through the Admin API.
  Divisions-only members are not blocked: the guard reads memberships on
  root nodes.
- **`pricing_access` rides the priced payloads** (2026-08-31,
  implementation): the code is a request-level fact injected through
  `serializer_params` and rendered on product, variant, cart, order and
  line-item payloads (null when prices are visible) — not a channel-only
  attribute a storefront would have to correlate across responses. The
  channel serializer already exposes the resolved `storefront_access`
  posture, unchanged.
- **Guests browse `approval_required` channels price-less** (2026-08-31,
  reaffirmed): catalog reads stay open with `pricing_access:
  'login_required'` for guests and `'company_required'` for signed-in
  buyers without active-company standing; only `login_required` rejects
  reads. The front door must be visible to prospects.
- **The dependency key is `company_activation_policy_class`** (2026-08-31):
  keeping the `_class` suffix in the key itself, exactly like
  `storefront_access_policy_class`, so consumers read
  `Spree.company_activation_policy_class`.
- **The storefront reference page (migration step 4) ships separately**
  (2026-08-31): the Next.js storefront lives in its own repository on
  `6-0-dev`; the monorepo delivery is core + API + SDK types + dashboard
  posture options + docs.

## Design Details

### Registration

`Companies::Register` workflow (`hooks :validate, :after_register`):
builds the root node + membership in one transaction, stamps
`metadata['registration']`, publishes `company.registered`. The `validate`
hook is the screening point (domain rules, fraud checks — host or
Enterprise handlers); it does NOT fire on `CompanyInvitations::Accept`
(joining an existing company is not founding one), while `Customers::Create`
hooks keep firing on both paths as today. Controller:
`POST /store/companies` (authenticated, rate-limited); the account
companies index and company serializer are unchanged except where the
policy adds codes.

One open guard: at most one self-registered root per customer per store
(validation in `Register`) — keeps duplicate applications out without a
uniqueness constraint on names.

### Activation policy

```ruby
class Spree::Companies::ActivationPolicy
  # Whether the company may act commercially (catalogs, pricing, checkout).
  def active?(_company) = true

  # The pricing_access code for a viewer lacking prices, or nil.
  def pricing_access_code(user:, store:)
    return 'login_required' if user.blank?
    'company_required' unless user.company_standing(store: store).any? { |c| active?(c) }
  end
end
```

Consulted (all existing call sites, no new resolution paths):
`Catalog.for_company` (assignments of active companies only), both
`sole_standing_company`s (memberships of active companies only),
`customer_has_standing_over_company` (cart write gains a distinct
`:not_active` error), the posture + checkout requirement. Completed orders
answer from their stamped `company_id`, untouched.

### Gating

`Channel::Gating::STOREFRONT_ACCESS += ['approval_required']`; store-level
fallback validation updated. `hide_prices?` becomes posture-aware; the
`pricing_access` code rides `serializer_params` beside `hide_prices` and is
exposed on the payloads that null prices.

## Migration Path

1. `ActivationPolicy` + dependency registration + the resolution call-site
   consultations (behavior-neutral under the default policy — specs assert
   that explicitly; shared-concern battery runs against Cart AND Order).
2. `approval_required` posture + `pricing_access` code + checkout
   requirement + serializer exposure.
3. `Companies::Register` + `POST /store/companies` + SDK types + docs.
4. Storefront reference: registration page + code-driven price labels.
5. Retire the Wholesale-group seed comment.

Nothing to migrate; every change is additive and inert until a merchant
selects the posture or registers a policy.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Any new code resolving a company for visibility, pricing, or checkout
  consults the activation policy** — and the two `sole_standing_company`
  implementations stay mirrored.
- **Never approval-gate self-service standing.**
- **New prices-hidden surfaces carry the `pricing_access` code** — no bare
  nulls.
- **No status/approval columns on `spree_companies`** — lifecycle state is
  the Enterprise application record's; OSS asks the policy.
- **Don't build interim approval primitives beside this mechanism** (customer-group
  membership, boolean flags); anything approval-shaped waits for this
  mechanism.

## Open Questions

None for the OSS scope. The flow-side questions (statuses, requirements,
