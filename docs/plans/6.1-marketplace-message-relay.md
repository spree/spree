# Marketplace Message Relay

**Status:** Draft
**Target:** Spree 6.1
**Depends on:** `6.0-multi-vendor-marketplace.md`, `6.0-seller-product-submission.md`
**Author:** Damian Legawiec
**Last updated:** 2026-08-26

## Summary

A seller packs and posts a parcel, so they need a way to reach the buyer when
something goes wrong with the delivery. They do not need the buyer's email
address to do it — that is the one contact detail that lets a marketplace's
customer be taken off the marketplace, and Spree now withholds it from the
seller order (`6.0-seller-product-submission.md`).

Today that leaves the phone on the shipping address as the only contact route.
This plan adds the second: a relayed message between buyer and seller where
neither learns the other's real address.

Rails ships the machinery. Action Mailbox is already required by the starter
app and unused; `has_secure_token` is the same primitive Spree uses for cart
tokens, invitations and digital links.

## Why this is 6.1 and not 6.0

The blocker is not the code, it is the deployment contract. Outbound mail in
Spree is env-driven SMTP (`SMTP_ADDRESS`, `SMTP_USERNAME`, …), so it works on
any host that can open a socket. Inbound mail is not like that: relaying
requires a public endpoint that a provider can POST to, MX records pointed at
that provider, and provider-specific ingress credentials.

Shipping this in core would mean every Spree installation carries a feature
most of them cannot turn on. It belongs behind a deliberate opt-in, and 6.0 is
already carrying the marketplace core.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

1. **Neither party learns the other's address.** A relayed message is sent
   *from* a per-thread alias on the marketplace's own domain and *to* the
   real recipient. The reply comes back to the alias, which is what the
   inbound route resolves. Masking one direction only would be theatre.

2. **The alias is an opaque token, never a derived string.** A relay address
   built from an order number or a seller slug is guessable, and a guessed
   alias is an unsolicited-mail channel into somebody's inbox.
   `has_secure_token` on the thread, exactly as `Spree::DigitalLink` and
   `Spree::CompanyInvitation` do it.

3. **The thread is the unit, not the message.** One `Spree::MessageThread` per
   (order, seller) pair holds the alias and both participants; messages hang
   off it. A per-message alias would multiply the addresses a leak could
   expose and give the buyer a different reply-to every time.

4. **A thread is bound to an order and expires with it.** Relay is for
   "where is my parcel", not a general channel — it opens when the order is
   placed and closes a fixed window after the order is fulfilled or canceled.
   An alias that never expires is a permanent forwarding address to a
   customer's inbox.

5. **Inbound is provider-agnostic.** Action Mailbox's own ingresses
   (Mailgun, Mandrill, Postmark, SendGrid, and the generic `relay` for a
   self-hosted MTA) are the supported surface. Spree registers a mailbox and
   documents the ingress choice; it does not wrap or prefer one provider.

6. **The relay is optional and off by default.** A store preference, and an
   installation that has not configured inbound mail simply does not offer
   it — the seller order keeps the shipping phone it already has. Nothing in
   fulfillment may depend on the relay existing.

7. **Messages are stored, not merely forwarded.** The marketplace is the one
   who has to answer "what did the seller tell my customer" in a dispute.
   A forward-only relay leaves the operator with nothing to look at.

## Design Details

### Models

- `Spree::MessageThread` — `store_id`, `order_id`, `seller_id`, `token`
  (`has_secure_token`), `status` (`open` | `closed`), `closes_at`.
- `Spree::Message` — `message_thread_id`, `sender_type` (`customer` |
  `seller` | `operator`), `sender_id`, `body`, `delivered_at`.

Both `Spree::SingleStoreResource`. `status` is a plain `has_status`, moved by
workflows, per `6.0-service-workflows.md` — no state machine.

### Outbound

`Spree::MessageMailer` sends the body to the counterparty with
`reply_to: "#{thread.token}@#{store.preferred_relay_domain}"`. The `from` is
the marketplace's own address, as every other Spree email is.

### Inbound

`Spree::RelayMailbox` routes on the alias:

- resolve the thread by token; unknown or closed token is dropped, never
  bounced (a bounce confirms to a guesser which aliases exist)
- identify the sender by the envelope against the thread's two participants;
  a third party is dropped
- store the message, then mail the counterparty

### API and panel

- Seller branch: `GET /seller/orders/:id/messages`, `POST .../messages`.
- Store API: the customer's side, on their own order.
- Admin: read-only across threads, for disputes.

## Migration Path

1. Models + workflows + mailer (works without inbound: the marketplace can
   send, the reply just has nowhere to land).
2. `Spree::RelayMailbox` + ingress documentation.
3. Seller panel and storefront surfaces.
4. Operator's read-only view.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Do not add the buyer's email back to any seller-facing serializer.** The
  relay is the replacement for it; putting the address back makes the relay
  pointless.
- **Nothing in fulfillment may require a thread to exist.** Relay is optional
  and a seller must be able to ship without it.
- A relay alias is never derived from anything guessable.

## Open Questions

- Whether the operator should be able to *post* into a thread or only read it.
  Reading covers disputes; posting makes the marketplace a party to the
  conversation, which may be what an operator wants and may be what they must
  avoid.
- Attachments. A delivery photograph is the obvious case and the obvious
  malware vector.
- Whether the storefront side is in scope for OSS at all, or whether a
  headless install is expected to render it from the API.
