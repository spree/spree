# Seller Order Management

**Status:** Draft — designed, nothing built
**Target:** Spree 6.0
**Depends on:** `6.0-multi-vendor-marketplace.md` (Seller identity, the `/api/v3/seller` branch, order splitting, the fund ledger — all shipped), `6.0-returns-exchanges-claims.md` (Return/Exchange/Claim + their fifteen workflows — shipped), `6.0-fulfillment-and-delivery.md` (Fulfillment status model, `Fulfillments::Fulfill`/`Cancel`/`Resume` — shipped), `6.0-admin-spa.md` (panel composition, the three-package split)
**Author:** Damian Legawiec
**Last updated:** 2026-09-05

## Summary

The seller panel's order screens are a hand-rolled minimum — a flat list of items, one tracking input, and a cancel button — while the operator dashboard grew a full order surface: a fulfillments card with carrier-aware tracking, partial fulfillment and splitting, and cards for returns, exchanges and claims. A seller running their own back office needs the same operations on their own orders, because on a marketplace the seller *is* the merchant of record for their child order: they pack it, post it, take the goods back and make the customer whole.

This plan rebuilds the seller order views to match the dashboard's, and adds the seller-branch endpoints they need. It is deliberately a port of an existing, proven surface rather than a new design: the operator dashboard's components are the reference, and the seller panel reproduces their structure and vocabulary with an operator-only subset removed.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

### 1. A seller may refund their own orders — restoring legacy behavior, and this amends the marketplace plan

`6.0-multi-vendor-marketplace.md` says twice that a seller gets no refunds — "What a seller may not do (publish to channels, edit orders, **refund**, approve itself…) is an endpoint that does not exist" (Decision 10) and "**No** refunds, order edits, approvals, payments" (the Phase 4 scope note). **Both are wrong about the module they are opening up, and this plan supersedes them on that one point.**

The legacy Enterprise multi-vendor module granted vendors the whole money-back surface, scoped to their own orders, in `SpreeMultiVendor::PermissionSets::VendorUser#apply_returns_permissions`:

```ruby
can :create, Spree::Refund
can :manage, Spree::Refund,        payment: { order: { vendor_id: @vendor_ids } }
can :create, Spree::Reimbursement
can :manage, Spree::Reimbursement, order: { vendor_id: @vendor_ids }
can :manage, Spree::ReturnAuthorization, order: { vendor_id: @vendor_ids }
can :manage, Spree::CustomerReturn,      stock_location: { vendor_id: @vendor_ids }
can :manage, Spree::ReturnItem,   inventory_unit: { order: { vendor_id: @vendor_ids } }
can :read,   Spree::RefundReason
can :read,   Spree::ReimbursementType
```

`Reimbursement` was 5.x's money-back mechanism and `Refund` is the gateway refund itself. The module shipped no return screens of its own — vendors reached the stock admin order tabs, narrowed by that permission set. So refunding is **shipped Enterprise behavior a marketplace operator already relies on**, not new latitude, and opening the marketplace core without it would be a regression for every migrating customer.

The commercial logic agrees. A seller is merchant of record for their child order (the marketplace plan's own transfer-basis decision: consumer tax and shipping revenue are theirs). The party who owes the customer their money back is the party who took it. Routing every seller refund through the operator makes the operator a ticket queue for other people's customer service, which is exactly the cost a marketplace exists to avoid.

**What bounds it is arithmetic, not permission.** Three bounds hold, and all three are already in the code:

- `Returns::Refund` refuses an amount above `return_record.refundable_total` (`refund_total - refunded_total`), and a return belongs to exactly one seller's order — so a seller can only ask for what their own order's returned units are worth.
- `Spree::Refund#order_is_covered_by_payment` validates *which* child a refund names — on a grouped payment the `order_id` is required and must be named by one of the payment's splits.
- `Spree::Refunds::Create#refundable_share` caps the amount at that child's captured share, counting refunds from the rows rather than the split's subscriber-written figure (so two refunds landing together cannot each see an unrefunded share) and re-checking under the payment's row lock. It is the only path that creates a `Spree::Refund` anywhere in the codebase, so nothing bypasses it.

Legacy needed none of this because each sub-order carried its own real `Payment` row, so `credit_allowed` was already per-vendor. In 6.0 payments hang off the `OrderGroup` and the split is what attributes money to a child — `refundable_share` is where that lands.

**Prerequisite (Decision 1a, revised 2026-09-03).** The bound was never the problem; *reaching the payment at all* was. `Returns::Refund`, `Claims::Resolve` and `Exchanges::Fulfill` all drained `order.payments.completed`, which is empty on a grouped child — so all three failed with `:no_refundable_payments` on exactly the orders a seller refunds, and no spec covered the grouped case in any of them. They now share `Spree::RefundsOrderPayments`, which refunds through the order's `payment_splits` when grouped (skipping a share mid-capture, as `Orders::Cancel` does) and through `order.payments` when not. This is a correctness fix for **operators** too — refunds, claims and exchanges were broken on every marketplace order — so it lands as its own commit ahead of any seller work.

The reversal path needs nothing new either: a refund on a fulfilled order already writes a negative `SellerTransfer` (`kind: 'refund_reversal'`, bounded by `reversible_amount`) through the shipped subscriber, so a seller refunding themselves correctly reduces their own payout.

**Still not the seller's:** the group's `Payment` (capture, void, adding a payment), order edits, approvals, and refunds on an order that is not theirs. Those remain endpoints that do not exist.

**Cancelling refunds too (amended 2026-09-05).** The seller cancel endpoint first shipped without `refund_payments`, on the reasoning that returning money already taken was the operator's call. That contradicted this very decision: a seller who withdraws from an order they cannot fulfil is the party who took that money, and routing the refund through the operator makes them a ticket queue exactly as described above. The endpoint now accepts `refund_payments`, and the seller panel offers the same switch the operator has.

The bound needs nothing new. `Orders::Cancel#amount_paid` sums only this order's `payment_splits` on a grouped order, and `settle_grouped_payments` refunds share by share, capped at each `net_captured_amount` and skipping any share mid-capture — so a seller cancelling can only reach their own child's money. `refund_amount` stays off the seller endpoint: withdrawing from the whole order returns what that order was paid, and a partial figure is a return, which has its own endpoint and its own ceiling.

### 2. The seller panel reproduces the dashboard's structure, minus operator-only cards

The order page becomes the same two-column `ResourceLayout` the dashboard uses, with the same card vocabulary in the same order. What a seller sees is a subset, decided per card by whether the seller owns the question:

| Card | Seller | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fulfillments (tracking, partial fulfill, split, cancel/resume) | yes | the seller packs and posts |
| Returns / Exchanges / Claims | yes | the seller takes the goods back and makes it right |
| Order summary (totals) | yes | already serialized, never rendered today |
| Items, both addresses, customer note | yes | merchant of record |
| Customer (name only) | yes, without email | see Decision 3 |
| Payments, tax lines, discounts, fees | no | the marketplace's money surface |
| Purchase order, internal note, tags, marketplace card, digital links, custom fields, metadata | no | operator bookkeeping |

The panel keeps composing from `@spree/dashboard-ui` and `@spree/dashboard-core` and does **not** import from `@spree/dashboard` — the operator app is not a library, and its components are wired to `adminClient`. Where a component is genuinely identical modulo its SDK, it is reimplemented against `sellerClient()` rather than parameterised across the two, because the two clients return different types and a shared generic component would have to be generic over both.

### 3. The customer's email stays hidden, and no new endpoint may leak it

`Seller::OrderSerializer` withholds the buyer's email deliberately, with the reasoning recorded in the class: it is the one contact detail that lets a marketplace's customer be taken off it, and packing, posting and invoicing need only the phone on the shipping address. `Spree::Exports::Orders::SELLER_OMITTED_HEADERS` drops it from the CSV for the same reason.

That rule now has to survive three new nested resources. Returns, exchanges and claims all reach `order.customer` transitively, so **the seller serializers for them are declared from `V3::BaseSerializer`, never subclassed from their admin counterparts**, and none of them expands a customer.

### 4. Reasons are read-only on the seller branch

Creating a return or a claim wants a reason, and the vocabulary (`Spree::ReturnReason`, `Spree::ClaimReason`) is the marketplace's to define — a `Spree::NamedType` the operator curates. Sellers get `GET /seller/return_reasons` and `GET /seller/claim_reasons`, index only, active rows, exactly as they already get `product_types` and `delivery_profiles`. No CRUD: a seller inventing private reasons would fragment the operator's reporting dimension.

### 5. Controllers are written scope-first, never subclassed from admin

The marketplace plan's Decision 10 is binding here: "Do **not** subclass admin controllers into the seller namespace — inherited `current_store.*` lookups are precisely the leak; share workflows and permitted-param lists, never controllers." Every new controller roots at `current_seller_orders` and reaches its records through the order, so a foreign id is a 404 by construction rather than a 403 by rule.

The workflows are shared unchanged — `Spree.return_*_workflow`, `Spree.claim_*_workflow`, `Spree.exchange_*_workflow`, `Spree.fulfillment_*_workflow` — so hooks, emails, tax credits and ledger reversals fire identically whoever triggers them.

### 6. No new permission keys

`Spree::Return`, `Spree::Exchange` and `Spree::Claim` are already subjects of the `orders` catalog resource, and `orders` is already `audiences: %i[seller]`. So `read_orders`/`write_orders` gate all of this, and the seeded default seller role (which holds `write_orders`) needs no change. `Spree::Refund` is a subject of the separate `refunds` resource, which is **not** seller-grantable — but a seller refunds *through* `Returns::Refund` on their own return, not through the refunds endpoint, so no key changes there either. Adding `refunds` to the seller audience would open `/admin`-shaped refund creation and is explicitly not part of this.

### 7. Seller users are already the right principal

`Return#created_by`, `Exchange#created_by` and `Claim#created_by` are typed `Spree.admin_user_class`, and a seller's team member **is** an `AdminUser` (marketplace plan, Decision 10 — one class, several hats). So attribution works with no model change; a return a seller files records that seller's user.

## Design Details

### API surface added to `/api/v3/seller`

Nested under the seller's own order, mirroring the admin routes minus the money-surface ones:

```ruby
resources :orders, only: [:index, :show] do
  member { patch :cancel }

  resources :fulfillments, only: [:index, :show], controller: 'orders/fulfillments' do
    member do
      patch :fulfill
      patch :update          # tracking number + carrier
      patch :cancel
      patch :resume
      patch :split
      patch :mark_delivered
    end
  end

  resources :returns, only: [:index, :show, :create], controller: 'orders/returns' do
    member { patch :approve; patch :receive; patch :refund; patch :cancel }
  end

  resources :exchanges, only: [:index, :show, :create], controller: 'orders/exchanges' do
    member { patch :approve; patch :receive; patch :fulfill; patch :cancel }
  end

  resources :claims, only: [:index, :show, :create], controller: 'orders/claims' do
    member { patch :approve; patch :resolve; patch :deny; patch :cancel }
  end
end

resources :return_reasons, only: [:index]
resources :claim_reasons,  only: [:index]
resources :tracking_carriers, only: [:index]
```

Deliberately absent from the fulfillments member list: `purchase_label`. Carrier accounts live on the store-scoped `Spree::Integration` and a seller method is internal-rate/manual-fulfillment only (marketplace plan, Decision 13), so a seller buys no labels in 6.0.

`update` on a fulfillment is narrowed to tracking: the admin endpoint also moves a parcel between stock locations and re-picks a delivery rate, and a seller's origin move is a different question that is not in this cut. Permitted params are `tracking` and `tracking_carrier` only.

### Serializers

New, all declared from `V3::BaseSerializer` and none expanding a customer: `Seller::ReturnSerializer`, `Seller::ReturnLineItemSerializer`, `Seller::ExchangeSerializer`, `Seller::ExchangeLineItemSerializer`, `Seller::ClaimSerializer`, `Seller::ClaimLineItemSerializer`, `Seller::ReasonSerializer` (one, serving both vocabularies — a reason is `id`/`name`/`active`).

`Seller::FulfillmentSerializer` gains nothing: it already carries `tracking`, `tracking_carrier`, `tracking_url`, `tracking_status`, `fulfilled_at`, `delivery_method_name` and `stock_location_name`, all of which the current UI ignores.

`Seller::OrderSerializer` gains the money fields the summary card needs that it does not already have (`shipping_total`, `promo_total`, `additional_tax_total`, `included_tax_total` and their `display_` twins), plus `canceled_at`. It does **not** gain `email`, `customer`, `payments`, `fees` or `discounts`.

### Panel structure

```
pages/order.tsx                        → composition only, like the dashboard's route
components/orders/order-header.tsx     → PageHeader + cancel in destructiveItems
components/orders/fulfillments-card.tsx
components/orders/fulfillment-fulfill-form.tsx
components/orders/fulfillment-tracking-dialog.tsx
components/orders/fulfillment-split-dialog.tsx
components/orders/fulfillment-item-list.tsx
components/orders/order-items-card.tsx
components/orders/order-summary-card.tsx
components/orders/order-customer-card.tsx      → name + addresses, no email
components/orders/returns-card.tsx
components/orders/post-sale-cards.tsx          → exchanges + claims
components/orders/post-sale-create-dialogs.tsx → create return/exchange/claim, resolve claim
hooks/use-order.ts, use-fulfillments.ts, use-returns.ts, use-post-sale.ts, use-reasons.ts
```

The seller panel has no `hooks/` directory today — every page inlines `useQuery`. This introduces one, matching the dashboard's convention, because eleven mutations across four resources inlined into cards is how the current page became unmaintainable.

### Orders list

Largely already right (it has the export button, quick filters and the status vocabularies). Two additions: a `status` quick filter so canceled orders can be found, and row selection is deliberately still **not** added — the dashboard's orders table has no bulk actions either.

## Migration Path

0. **Grouped-order refunds (Decision 1a), not additive.** `Spree::RefundsOrderPayments` + `PaymentSplit#refundable_amount`, adopted by `Returns::Refund`, `Claims::Resolve` and `Exchanges::Fulfill`, with a spec covering the grouped case for all three. Ships first and separately — it corrects operator refunds as well, and the seller endpoint cannot work without it.
1. **Backend, additive only.** Routes, six controllers (`orders/returns`, `orders/exchanges`, `orders/claims`, `return_reasons`, `claim_reasons`, `tracking_carriers`), seven serializers, the fulfillments controller's four new member actions. Specs per controller: a happy path, a cross-seller 404, and the money bound on refund.
2. **SDK.** `seller-sdk` gains `orders.returns`, `orders.exchanges`, `orders.claims`, `returnReasons`, `claimReasons`, `trackingCarriers`, and the new fulfillment verbs. Regenerate types (`typelizer:generate`).
3. **Panel.** Port the components in the order listed above; the fulfillments card first, since it is what a seller touches daily.
4. **OpenAPI + i18n.** Integration specs for the new endpoints, `swaggerize`, and every new key into all six locale files.

No data migration: everything here is additive, and no existing column, route or serializer field changes meaning.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Never subclass an admin controller into the seller namespace.** Root at `current_seller_orders` and reach nested records through the order.
- **A seller serializer is never a subclass of an admin or store one.** Declare from `V3::BaseSerializer`; a subclass inherits fields (email, payments, cost price) that the seller branch must not carry.
- **No new seller endpoint may expose the buyer's email**, directly or through an expansion. If a new surface needs to contact the customer, it uses the phone on the shipping address.
- **Money moves only through the shared workflows**, never a hand-rolled refund. `Refunds::Create` owns the per-child ceiling, the row lock and the gateway credit; a call site that writes a `Spree::Refund` another way is a cross-seller money bug.
- **`payment.credit_allowed` is group-wide on a grouped payment** — it sums refunds across every child order, so it is never a per-seller ceiling. The child's share is `PaymentSplit#refundable_amount`.
- **Never read `order.payments` on a path that may see a grouped order** — it is empty there. Use `order.payment_splits` (or `Order#settlement_payments` where a payment list is genuinely wanted); a new post-sale money path uses `Spree::RefundsOrderPayments`.
- **Do not add `refunds`, `payments` or `commissions` to the seller audience** in the permission catalog. A seller refunds through their own return, and that is the only path.
- **No label purchase on the seller branch** while carrier credentials are store-scoped.
- The marketplace plan's "no refunds" lines are superseded by Decision 1 here; update them rather than re-deriving the old conclusion.

## Open Questions

1. **Should a seller see the customer's name at all?** This plan says yes (it is on the addresses regardless, and a packing slip carries it), but a stricter marketplace might want initials only. Deferred: it is a serializer field, changeable without restructuring.
2. **Exchange fulfillment origin.** `Exchanges::Fulfill` builds replacement fulfillments; which of the seller's stock locations they draw from is currently the order's default. Worth revisiting when seller pickup lands.
3. **Cross-order returns list for sellers.** The operator has read-only `/returns`, `/exchanges`, `/claims` pages. A seller arguably wants the same "what is open right now" view; left out of this cut because the per-order cards answer the daily question first.
