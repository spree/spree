# Shipping Labels and Deliveries

**Status:** Draft — design finalized 2026-09-03, nothing built
**Target:** Spree 6.0
**Depends on:** `6.0-fulfillment-and-delivery.md` (status model, `PurchaseLabel`, `UpdateTracking`), `6.0-delivery-rate-provider.md` (EasyPost reference provider), `6.0-returns-exchanges-claims.md` (the `Return` owner). Coordinates with: `6.0-b2b-wholesale-shipping.md` (freight consignments are deliveries too; freight paperwork is not a label), `6.1-b2b-order-documents.md` (where a bill of lading goes).
**Author:** Damian Legawiec
**Last updated:** 2026-09-03

## Summary

A shipping label is today three provider-private keys written into
`fulfillment.metadata` by the EasyPost gem, surfaced through a per-read
`provider.documents(fulfillment)` call, and bought or refunded through a
`PATCH .../purchase_label` member action. That leaves Spree not knowing what the
merchant paid the carrier, unable to reprint once the carrier's hosted URL is
gone, unable to buy a second label after a refund, and with nowhere to put a
return label. The carrier journey of a parcel is in the same shape: one tracking
number and one carrier status per fulfillment, in seven columns on
`spree_fulfillments`, with a `spree_fulfillment_trackings` model deferred to 6.1
and required to absorb that whole axis when it comes.

This plan promotes both into first-class records now, while 6.0 is still the
breaking window. **`Spree::ShippingLabel`** is a purchased (or uploaded)
carrier document with a cost, a file in private storage, a lifecycle
(`purchased → refund_requested → refunded`) and a polymorphic `owner` — a
`Fulfillment` for outbound parcels, a `Return` for inbound ones.
**`Spree::Delivery`** is one tracked consignment's carrier journey — a parcel,
or three pallets under one PRO number — with tracking number, carrier,
tracking status, estimate and arrival time, the same polymorphic owner, and
optionally born from a label. The fulfillment keeps its merchant lifecycle
(`unfulfilled → fulfilled → delivered`) and rolls `delivered` up from its
deliveries. Both become nested REST resources under the fulfillment in the
Admin API; `purchase_label` and `mark_delivered` stop being the only doors.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **The label is a record, the delivery is a record, delivered stays a
  transition (2026-09-03, probed and confirmed).** `POST .../deliveries` was
  considered as the way to say "it arrived" and rejected, because a delivery
  row is born when a tracking number is attached — at fulfil time or label
  purchase, days before anything arrives — so `POST` already means "this
  fulfilment went out as another consignment". Arrival is a state change on
  an existing row, and every lifecycle transition in the Admin API
  (`fulfill`, `cancel`, `capture`, `approve`, `receive`) is a `PATCH` member
  action. The fulfilment-level `mark_delivered` stays beside a per-delivery
  twin for three reasons: some fulfilments have no delivery rows at all
  (pickup, hand delivery, freight with no carrier feed) and still need to
  become `delivered` for the returns window; staff saying "it arrived"
  outranks the carrier and closes every open consignment in one call; and
  `Fulfillments::MarkDelivered` already carries public hooks. The alternative
  — redefining a delivery as a *handover* so a pickup gets a row with no
  tracking number and `delivered_at` set, making the status a pure rollup —
  was rejected because that row would be a flag wearing a table.
- **`Spree::Delivery` is the name (2026-09-03, revisited twice).** The row
  must be modality-neutral: `6.0-b2b-wholesale-shipping.md` uses "parcel" as
  the antonym of freight ("different from parcel shipping"), and a freight
  consignment is one PRO or container number covering several pallets, so
  `Spree::Parcel` — briefly chosen — contradicted the sibling plan's fixed
  vocabulary. `Spree::Tracking` is the tracking industry's own resource name
  but collides permanently with `Fulfillment#tracking`, the 5.6 string field
  that stays; `TrackingStatus` names one column of the row; `FulfillmentTracking`
  (the 6.1 deferral's name) is wrong for a `Return` owner. Carriers say
  "track your delivery" for a parcel and a pallet alike. Accepted trade-off:
  the word now carries two meanings in the codebase — `DeliveryMethod`,
  `DeliveryRate`, `DeliveryProfile` and `DeliveryZone` describe what a store
  offers, a `Delivery` is one consignment actually travelling. Workflows live
  under `Spree::Deliveries::`, which no existing namespace uses.
- **Polymorphic `owner` on both tables (2026-09-03).** One concept — a carrier
  document, a consignment in flight — held by different kinds of party, which
  is the 2026-08-22 tax-identifier ruling, not the dual-FK case (a type string
  hiding several meanings). `Fulfillment` and `Return` are the 6.0 owners;
  exchanges ship their replacements through fulfillments, so they need no
  owner of their own. Cardinality is declared owner-side: a fulfillment
  `has_many` of each, a return `has_many` of each.
- **The delivery absorbs the entire carrier axis (honours decisions.md
  2026-08-11).** `tracking`, `tracking_carrier`, `tracking_status`,
  `tracking_details`, `estimated_delivery_at` move to `spree_deliveries`.
  Webhooks match a delivery row, `UpdateTracking` takes a `delivery:`, and a
  fulfillment is `delivered` when every one of its deliveries is — or when
  staff say so for a fulfillment that has none. `delivered_at` on the
  fulfillment stays as the lifecycle stamp (the returns window and withdrawal
  anchor), exactly like `fulfilled_at`: it is when the fulfillment became
  delivered, a rollup, not a second copy of the carrier's report.
- **The fulfillment keeps `tracking` and `tracking_url`, nothing else
  (2026-09-03).** Those two fields shipped in the 5.6 Store API, so they stay
  permanently as a read-only summary of the **primary delivery** (the
  earliest row) — single-parcel storefronts and the fulfilled email stay
  one-field simple, and `tracking:` (with `tracking_carrier:`) on
  `Fulfillments::Fulfill`/`Update` stays as the write shortcut that creates
  or corrects that delivery. Everything the 6.0 edge added to the
  fulfillment — `tracking_carrier`, `tracking_carrier_name`,
  `tracking_status`, `tracking_details`, `estimated_delivery_at` — is
  **removed** from the serializers and the table rather than bridged: it was
  never released, so there is nobody to bridge for, and the four columns come
  out of their own 20260811 migrations in place (the unreleased-edge rule the
  catalog and company work already used). `delivered_at` and `fulfilled_at`
  stay: they are lifecycle stamps, not carrier data.
- **One label, at most one delivery (2026-09-03).** A purchased label mints a
  delivery carrying its tracking number; the delivery points back through
  `shipping_label_id` (unique). Hand-entered tracking is a delivery with no
  label. A refunded label's delivery is removed only if the consignment never
  moved (the fulfillment is still `unfulfilled`); once fulfilled, the journey
  is history and stays.
- **Idempotency moves into core (2026-09-03).** `Fulfillments::PurchaseLabel`
  refuses when the owner already has a non-refunded label. Providers no longer
  need `metadata` keys to avoid double-buying; the contract for
  `create_fulfillment` idempotency (decisions.md 2026-08-12) is retired
  because the workflow never asks twice.
- **Purchase is a typed result, never a hash (2026-09-03).** A label-generating
  provider implements `purchase_label(owner)` returning a
  `Spree::LabelPurchase` value object (`ActiveModel::Model` + `Attributes`:
  `external_id`, `carrier`, `service`, `tracking_number`, `tracking_url`,
  `cost`, `currency`, `format`, `file_url`, `metadata`) and
  `refund_label(shipping_label)` returning the resulting status.
  `create_fulfillment`/`cancel_fulfillment` stay for providers whose dispatch
  is not a label (a 3PL pick, a pickup counter). `documents(owner)` stays for
  documents that are not the label — customs forms, commercial invoices —
  and stops reporting the label.
- **The file lives in Spree (2026-09-03).** Right after purchase the label
  file is fetched from the provider's URL into a private-storage attachment on
  the row (same declaration and spoofing protection as `po_document`). The
  download endpoint streams the attachment, and proxies the provider URL only
  while the fetch is still pending. Merchants reprint without the carrier
  account being connected, and a disconnected integration never breaks a
  warehouse.
- **Cost is recorded, never displayed to the shopper (2026-09-03).** `cost` +
  `currency` on the label is what the merchant paid the carrier — accounting
  data, admin-only. It never touches the order's shipping charge (`cost` on
  the fulfillment is what the customer was charged) and never appears on a
  store serializer.
- **Uploaded labels are in scope (2026-09-03).** Merchants without a carrier
  integration buy postage elsewhere and still need the PDF and the cost on
  the consignment. `POST .../labels` with a signed blob id records a
  `source: 'uploaded'` label (tracking number, carrier, cost, currency) and
  mints its delivery through `ShippingLabels::Record`; the same request
  without a file purchases through the provider. The serializer's `source`
  tells them apart, and refund is refused on an uploaded label (nothing to
  refund — the merchant deletes it instead, which is the one destroy the
  resource allows). Sellers get upload but never purchase.
- **Freight paperwork is not a shipping label (2026-09-03, wholesale
  cross-check).** A bill of lading, a pallet placard or a packing list is
  issued by the forwarder or printed by the merchant; it has no cost to
  refund and no carrier lifecycle. It goes to `Spree::OrderDocument`
  (`6.1-b2b-order-documents.md`) or the client-side prints, never to a
  `source: 'uploaded'` label. What freight *does* share with parcels is the
  delivery row: the forwarder's PRO or container number is hand-entered as a
  delivery with a free-text `carrier` and a pasted `tracking_url`, and
  `delivered` is staff-confirmed through `mark_delivered` — which is exactly
  why that fulfilment-level action stays.
- **Return labels ship in this plan (2026-09-03).** The `Return` owner is
  what makes the polymorphic shape earn its keep, so it is written in 6.0:
  `Returns::PurchaseLabel` (thin, like the fulfillment twin) over
  `ShippingLabels::Purchase(owner: return_record)`, the EasyPost provider
  buying an `is_return` shipment from the customer's address back to the
  return's stock location, the same `labels` controller mounted under
  `/orders/:order_id/returns/:return_id/`, a customer download on the Store
  API, and a `Return` delivery for the inbound parcel whose `delivered`
  report **never** auto-receives — `Returns::Receive` stays a staff act
  because arrival is not inspection. The returns plan's interim "URL in
  `metadata`" advice is withdrawn.
- **No transition graph, no state machine.** `ShippingLabel` uses
  `has_status`; every status change is a workflow (`ShippingLabels::Purchase`,
  `::Refund`, `::Record` for uploads). `Delivery#status` is the carrier's
  vocabulary and stays data written by `Deliveries::UpdateTracking` — a
  `pending` value replaces today's `nil` so the creating workflow sets it and
  the column never carries a database default.

## Design Details

### Models

**`Spree::ShippingLabel`** (`spree_shipping_labels`, prefix `lbl`,
`Spree::SingleStoreResource`, `Spree::Metadata`, `has_status :purchased,
:refund_requested, :refunded`)

| Column | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| `owner_type`, `owner_id` | polymorphic, `null: false`, indexed together |
| `store_id` | `null: false` |
| `integration_id` | nullable — the connected carrier account that sold it; nil for uploaded labels |
| `source` | `purchased` \| `uploaded`, `null: false` |
| `external_id` | provider's shipment/label id |
| `carrier`, `service` | strings, carrier key from `Spree.tracking_carriers` when known |
| `tracking_number` | the number printed on the label — a frozen purchase fact; the delivery holds the live one |
| `cost`, `currency` | `decimal(10,2)` default 0 + string; what the merchant paid |
| `format` | `pdf` \| `png` \| `zpl` |
| `status` | string, set by the workflow |
| `refunded_at` | datetime |
| `metadata` | JSON — provider ids (tracker id, original file URL) |
| `file` | `has_one_attached`, `Spree.private_storage_service_name`, content type limited to PDF/PNG/ZPL, spoofing-protected |

Associations: `belongs_to :owner, polymorphic: true`, `belongs_to :store`,
`belongs_to :integration, optional: true`, `has_one :delivery`. Scopes:
`active` (`where.not(status: 'refunded')`). `Fulfillment has_many
:shipping_labels, as: :owner, dependent: :destroy`; `Return` the same.

**`Spree::Delivery`** (`spree_deliveries`, prefix `dlv`,
`Spree::SingleStoreResource`)

| Column | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| `owner_type`, `owner_id` | polymorphic, `null: false` |
| `store_id` | `null: false` |
| `shipping_label_id` | nullable, unique index — the label that minted it |
| `tracking_number` | `null: false`, indexed (webhook lookup); unique per owner. A parcel's carrier number, or a freight PRO / container number |
| `tracking_url` | nullable — a pasted link or the provider's tracker page; the resolved URL falls back to the carrier template then format detection, as today |
| `carrier` | free-text string. Detected from the number when not given (today's `detect_tracking_carrier`); `Spree.tracking_carriers` supplies URL templates and display names, never validation — a forwarder's name is a legal value |
| `service` | string, nullable |
| `status` | `pending` \| `pre_transit` \| `in_transit` \| `out_for_delivery` \| `available_for_pickup` \| `delivered` \| `return_to_sender` \| `failure` \| `unknown` — the carrier axis, inclusion-validated, no graph |
| `estimated_delivery_at`, `delivered_at` | datetimes |
| `details` | JSON — the carrier payload worth keeping (scan history, signature, failure reason) |

Associations: `belongs_to :owner, polymorphic: true`, `belongs_to :store`,
`belongs_to :shipping_label, optional: true`. `Fulfillment has_many
:deliveries, as: :owner, dependent: :destroy` (ordered by `created_at`; the
first is the **primary delivery** the summary fields read). `Return` the same.

### Fulfillment lifecycle with deliveries

- `Fulfillments::Fulfill` — `tracking:` (and `tracking_carrier:`) still
  accepted as the one-parcel shortcut: it creates a delivery through
  `Deliveries::Create` inside the same transaction as the split. The label
  step becomes `external_step :purchase_label` calling
  `ShippingLabels::Purchase` when the provider generates labels and no active
  label exists; failure still degrades to "no label yet". The fulfilled email
  reads tracking from the primary delivery.
- `Fulfillments::PurchaseLabel` — thin: validate, refuse on an active label,
  delegate to `ShippingLabels::Purchase`. Kept as a workflow because its hooks
  (`validate`, `after_purchase_label`) are public since 2026-08-12.
- `Fulfillments::Update` — `tracking:` upserts the primary delivery (creates
  it, or corrects its number; a corrected number resets `status` to
  `pending`).
- `Fulfillments::Cancel` — `tell_provider_to_stand_down` refunds every active
  label through `ShippingLabels::Refund` (`notify_provider: false` still lets
  `Orders::Cancel` batch the carrier I/O), then the 3PL `cancel_fulfillment`.
- `Fulfillments::MarkDelivered` — staff confirmation. Marks every
  non-delivered delivery `delivered` at the given time (a human saying "it
  arrived" outranks a carrier that never sent the last scan), then the
  fulfillment. Unchanged signature. This is the freight path: no webhook ever
  arrives for a container.
- `Deliveries::UpdateTracking` (renamed from `Fulfillments::UpdateTracking`;
  `fulfillment_update_tracking_workflow` stays readable one release and
  resolves the primary delivery) — writes the carrier axis onto one row with
  `update_columns`, as today. When the report is `delivered` it stamps the row
  and, if every sibling delivery is delivered, delegates to
  `Fulfillments::MarkDelivered` with the latest `delivered_at`. A `Return`
  owner never auto-transitions anything: goods arriving is not goods
  inspected, and `Returns::Receive` stays a staff act. A per-delivery
  `mark_delivered` from staff is this workflow with `tracking_status:
  'delivered'`.
- `Deliveries::Create` / `Deliveries::Destroy` — Tier 1 services (no hook
  earned). Destroy refuses a delivery that has a label — refund the label.

### Label workflows

- `ShippingLabels::Purchase(owner:)` — `validate` hook, `ensure_purchasable`
  (provider `generates_labels?`, owner not fulfilled/canceled, no active
  label, order not draft), `external_step :purchase` (provider
  `purchase_label(owner)` → `LabelPurchase`), then in one transaction create
  the label row and its delivery, then `external_step :store_file` (fetch
  `file_url` into the attachment; on failure enqueue
  `ShippingLabels::StoreFileJob` and leave the URL in `metadata`). Publishes
  `shipping_label.purchased`. Hook `after_purchase`.
- `ShippingLabels::Refund(shipping_label:)` — `validate`, `ensure_refundable`
  (status `purchased`, source `purchased`), `external_step :refund` (provider
  `refund_label` → `refund_requested` or `refunded`), write the status,
  remove the delivery when the owner never shipped. Publishes
  `shipping_label.refunded`. A provider whose refund resolves later reports
  it through its webhook by calling `ShippingLabels::ConfirmRefund` (plain
  service).
- `ShippingLabels::Record(owner:, file:, tracking_number:, carrier:, cost:,
  currency:)` — an uploaded label: creates a `source: 'uploaded'` row and
  its delivery, no provider involved.

### Provider contract (`Spree::FulfillmentProvider::Base`)

```ruby
def self.generates_labels? = false           # unchanged
def purchase_label(owner)                     # → Spree::LabelPurchase | nil
def refund_label(shipping_label)              # → 'refunded' | 'refund_requested' | false
def create_fulfillment(fulfillment)           # unchanged — non-label dispatch
def cancel_fulfillment(fulfillment)           # unchanged
def documents(owner)                          # unchanged shape, label excluded
def tracking_url(delivery)                    # was tracking_url(fulfillment); deprecated twin one release
```

The EasyPost provider implements `purchase_label` from today's
`buy_quoted`/`requote_and_buy` (returning `postage_label.label_url`,
`rate.rate`/`currency`, `label_file_type`; `is_return: true` when the owner
is a `Return`, shipping from the customer's address to the return's stock
location), `refund_label` from `cancel_fulfillment`, drops `remember_purchase`
and every `metadata['easypost_*']` read, and keeps `documents` for
`shipment.forms`. Its tracker webhook resolves the delivery by tracking
number scoped to the integration's store. The freight rate provider from the
wholesale plan pairs with the Manual fulfilment provider and generates no
labels; its consignments are hand-entered deliveries.

### Admin API (`spree/api`)

Nested under the order's fulfillment (there is no top-level fulfillments
resource in the Admin API). Both ride the `fulfillments` permission resource —
their subjects join its list.

```
GET    /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/labels
POST   /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/labels           buy through the provider; with `file` (signed blob id), record an uploaded label
GET    /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/labels/:id
GET    /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/labels/:id/download   streamed, read scope
PATCH  /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/labels/:id/refund
DELETE /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/labels/:id        uploaded labels only

GET    /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/deliveries
POST   /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/deliveries       tracking_number, carrier, tracking_url
GET    /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/deliveries/:id
PATCH  /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/deliveries/:id   correct number / carrier / url
DELETE /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/deliveries/:id   422 when it has a label
PATCH  /api/v3/admin/orders/:order_id/fulfillments/:fulfillment_id/deliveries/:id/mark_delivered
```

`PATCH .../fulfillments/:id/purchase_label` stays one release as a deprecated
alias of `POST .../labels` returning the fulfillment. `mark_delivered` on the
fulfillment is unchanged. The same `labels` controller mounts under
`/orders/:order_id/returns/:return_id/` for return labels.

Serializers: `Admin::ShippingLabelSerializer` (`id`, `owner_id`, `source`,
`status`, `carrier`, `service`, `tracking_number`, `cost`, `currency`,
`display_cost`, `format`, `download_url`, `refunded_at`, `metadata`,
timestamps) and `Admin::DeliverySerializer` extending a store
`DeliverySerializer` (`id`, `tracking_number`, `tracking_url`, `carrier`,
`carrier_name`, `status`, `estimated_delivery_at`, `delivered_at`; admin adds
`shipping_label_id`, `details`, timestamps). The fulfillment serializers embed
`deliveries` always (small, and the customer's question), keep `tracking` and
`tracking_url` as the primary delivery's summary, lose `tracking_carrier`,
`tracking_carrier_name`, `tracking_status`, `tracking_details` and
`estimated_delivery_at`; the admin one embeds `labels`; `documents` and
`provider_generates_labels` stay. Fulfillment write params keep `tracking` and
`tracking_carrier` (forwarded to the primary delivery) and nothing else
tracking-shaped.

Seller branch: sellers fulfill on manual methods only (Decision 13 of the
marketplace plan), so they get `deliveries` index/create/update/destroy and
`labels` index/upload/download/destroy under
`/seller/orders/:id/fulfillments/:id/` — never purchase or refund, which need
the operator's carrier account.

### Store API

`fulfillment.deliveries[]` on the order serializer beside the permanent
`tracking`/`tracking_url` summary. The only label surface is the customer's
return label: `GET /store/orders/:order_id/returns/:id/label` streams the
active label's file to the order's customer (or order-token holder) — the
label row itself is never serialized to the storefront, since its cost and
provider ids are merchant data.

### Dashboard

The fulfillment card lists deliveries (one badge per consignment with its
carrier status; "Add tracking" opens a sheet; a delivery with a label is not
deletable), renders the label row (Buy label → `POST labels`; Print →
`download`; Refund with a destructive confirm; Upload label for manual
methods), and shows postage cost. Return page gets the same label block.
Every string through i18next in all locales.

### Events

`shipping_label.purchased`, `shipping_label.refunded` (new, with an event
serializer). `fulfillment.delivered` unchanged. No per-scan delivery event —
the `after_update_tracking` hook is that surface, as today.

## Migration Path

1. **Schema** — one migration creating `spree_shipping_labels` and
   `spree_deliveries`. The two 20260811 fulfillment migrations are amended
   in place to stop adding `tracking_status`, `tracking_details`,
   `estimated_delivery_at` and `tracking_carrier` (unreleased edge — a dev
   database that already has them keeps harmless dead columns until
   `pnpm wt:template` rebuilds it). `spree_fulfillments.tracking` and its
   index stay: they are the 5.6 data the task below reads.
2. **Core** — models, `LabelPurchase`, workflows and services, provider base
   contract, `Fulfill`/`Update`/`Cancel`/`MarkDelivered`/`PurchaseLabel`
   rewired, `Deliveries::UpdateTracking`, `tracking`/`tracking_url` readers
   delegating to the primary delivery, `Spree::Dependencies` keys
   (`shipping_label_purchase_workflow`, `shipping_label_refund_workflow`,
   `shipping_label_record_workflow`, `delivery_update_tracking_workflow`,
   `return_purchase_label_workflow`; legacy
   `fulfillment_update_tracking_workflow` readable one release).
3. **Data task** — `spree:upgrade:migrate_deliveries` in the 5.6→6.0 manifest
   after `migrate_fulfillment_statuses`: every fulfillment with a `tracking`
   gets one `pending` delivery row carrying that number (a pasted link lands
   in `tracking_url`, the carrier is detected from the number as today);
   idempotent by `(owner, tracking_number)` existence. No label backfill: the
   EasyPost `metadata` keys only ever existed on the unreleased 6.0 edge.
4. **EasyPost** — `purchase_label` for both owners (`is_return` for a
   `Return`), `refund_label`, webhook matching by delivery, `documents`
   reduced to forms.
5. **API + SDK** — fulfillment- and return-nested `labels`, `deliveries`,
   store return-label download, serializers, typelizer/Zod/OpenAPI,
   admin-sdk and sdk resources, seller branch.
6. **Dashboard** — fulfillment card rework, return page label block, e2e.
7. **6.1** — drop `spree_fulfillments.tracking` and its index, remove the
   deprecated `purchase_label` alias, `fulfillment_update_tracking_workflow`
   and `tracking_url(fulfillment)`.

Phases 1–6 are one PR train on one branch; the data task ships with phase 3.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Never write `spree_fulfillments.tracking*`, `estimated_delivery_at` or
  `tracking_details` in new code** — tracking is a `Delivery` row created
  through `Deliveries::Create` (or the `tracking:` shortcut on
  `Fulfillments::Fulfill`/`Update`, which does the same). `delivered_at` on
  the fulfillment is written only by `Fulfillments::MarkDelivered`.
- **No provider reads or writes `fulfillment.metadata` for label state.**
  Label facts live on `Spree::ShippingLabel`; a provider that needs to
  remember its own ids puts them in the label's `metadata`.
- **A label's `cost` is merchant accounting, never shopper money** — it never
  feeds `fulfillment.cost`, totals, or a store serializer.
- **Webhook matching resolves a `Delivery`, scoped to the integration's
  store** — never `Fulfillment.where(tracking:)`.
- **Return labels attach to the `Return` as its `owner`** — no
  `return_label_url` column, no URL in `metadata` (the returns plan's interim
  advice is withdrawn once this ships).
- **`Fulfillment#delivered` is a rollup**: a new owner or a new carrier
  integration may never mark a fulfillment delivered directly — it reports a
  delivery and lets `Deliveries::UpdateTracking` roll up.
- **No new readers of `tracking_status`, `tracking_carrier`,
  `estimated_delivery_at` or `tracking_details` on a fulfillment** — the
  storefront, emails and dashboard read `deliveries`; a fulfillment only ever
  summarizes `tracking` and `tracking_url`.
- **`Delivery#carrier` is free text.** `Spree.tracking_carriers` supplies
  URL templates and display names; nothing may validate a carrier against
  it, or a forwarder's PRO number becomes unenterable.
- **Freight paperwork is never a `ShippingLabel`.** A bill of lading,
  pallet placard or packing list goes to `Spree::OrderDocument` or the
  client-side prints (`6.1-b2b-order-documents.md`); the label table holds
  carrier labels only.
- **A `Return` delivery never transitions the return.** Anything that wants
  "the parcel came back" to do something hangs it on
  `Deliveries::UpdateTracking`'s `after_update_tracking` hook and calls
  `Returns::Receive` itself, staff-style.
- **Label files are private-storage attachments streamed by the API** —
  never a public blob URL on any serializer, never `attachment.url`.

## Open Questions

None blocking. Three items are deliberately deferred:

- **Refund outcome from the carrier.** `ShippingLabels::Refund` records what
  the provider answers synchronously (`refunded` or `refund_requested`). A
  provider that settles refunds later (EasyPost's `refund.successful` /
  `refund.rejected` events) can complete the story through
  `ShippingLabels::ConfirmRefund`, but a `refund_rejected` status and the
  webhook wiring wait for a merchant to ask — unblocked by that request.
- **Several labels for one delivery** (a multi-piece shipment under one
  tracking number). Not modeled: one label mints one delivery. Unblocked by
  a carrier provider that actually returns multi-piece labels.
- **Packaging on the delivery.** The wholesale plan names its `pallet` and
  `container` `PackageType` rows as "the natural anchor for future
  label/BOL work"; a nullable `package_type_id` (plus a count) on
  `spree_deliveries` is the additive hook — "3 pallets under PRO 4471923".
  Unblocked by the wholesale plan landing and a merchant needing the
  consignment's packaging recorded, not just its number.
