## Summary

Add conditional availability rules to `Spree::PaymentMethod`: STI rule records (`Spree::PaymentMethodRule`) attached to a payment method that gate whether it is offered for a given checkout — by channel, market, order total, customer group, or company. This closes the gap surfaced by merchant asks ("wholesale buyers pay on terms while retail customers pay with Stripe", "orders over 5k qualify for installments", "market-bound payment methods") and confirmed by the 2026-07-23 competitive review: Vendure gates payment methods per channel plus a `PaymentMethodEligibilityChecker` (their canonical example is an order-total minimum), Saleor resolves payment apps per channel, and Shopify covers the same ground with Payment Customization Functions (cart total, market, B2B company inputs) plus, since May 2026, per-market business entities on Shopify Payments (Plus-gated).

The same mechanism answers the second merchant ask, **multiple accounts of one provider in one store** — a Stripe account for the EU markets and another for the US. The 2026-11-02 review settled that no new concept is needed: an account is simply a second `PaymentMethod` record of the same provider, and a `MarketRule` on each decides which one a checkout sees. An open-source platform's hosted payments product shipped exactly this decomposition in October 2026 (one provider instance registered per account, scoped by region, no new data model), and Shopify's multi-entity model is one Shopify Payments account per legal entity mapped to markets. What remains for Spree is plumbing, delivered as Phase 2 of this plan: the storefront must receive each method's public key from the API instead of an environment variable, the dashboard must stop hiding a provider once one record exists, and marketplace payouts must sign transfers with the account that took the charge.

This **supersedes half of a key decision** in `5.6-6.0-single-store-promotions-payment-methods.md` ("promotions and payment methods have no distribution concept, so the FK is the whole story"): the single-store FK stays correct, but payment methods do have an *eligibility* dimension within a store. Rules are that dimension — mirroring how promotions already solve the identical problem (`Spree::Promotion::Rules::Channel/Market/CustomerGroup/ItemTotal`), rather than adding scoping columns or join tables to `PaymentMethod`.

## Problem

1. **No channel dimension.** Checkout availability is `Spree::Purchase::PaymentProcessing#payment_methods` — `store.payment_methods.active.storefront_visible` filtered by `available_for_order?` — so every active storefront-visible payment method appears on every channel. A store running a B2C channel and a gated wholesale channel (seeded by default since 5.6) cannot offer invoice or bank-transfer payment to wholesale buyers without also showing it at B2C checkout.
2. **No market dimension.** Payment methods that only make sense for certain geographies (local methods, region-specific providers, a provider account that settles in one currency) cannot be bound to a Market, even though Markets already drive currency, locale and tax per request.
3. **No conditional rules.** "Installments only above 5k", "invoice only for the Wholesale customer group", "these companies pay on terms" have no home. The only seam is the per-gateway `available_for_order?` override — code, not configuration, and invisible to admins.
4. **One account per provider per store, enforced by the UI alone.** Two `SpreeStripe::Gateway` records in one store already work at the data layer: each carries its own keys, its own webhook endpoint (`/api/v3/webhooks/payments/:payment_method_id`) and signing secret, its own payment sessions and its own gateway customers. But the admin `types` endpoint marks a provider `installed` and the dashboard hides installed providers from the create picker and the Integrations gallery, so a merchant cannot add the second record — and without rules, a second record would appear as a second "Stripe" button on every checkout.
5. **The storefront loads Stripe with one publishable key from `NEXT_PUBLIC_STRIPE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`.** The Store API payment method serializer exposes name, description, type and two booleans; core has a `public_preferences` hook on the model that no gateway declares and no serializer emits. Two accounts need the key to travel with the method the cart was offered, or intents created on one account are confirmed with the other's key and every checkout on the second account fails.
6. **Marketplace payouts pick the store's first Stripe method.** `SpreeStripe::PayoutProvider.gateway_for(store)` returns the first active Stripe gateway, so with two accounts a transfer for an order charged on the second is signed with the first's key — which Stripe refuses, because a transfer must name a charge on the same account.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **Mirror `Spree::PromotionRule`, don't invent a framework.** STI base `Spree::PaymentMethodRule` on a new `spree_payment_method_rules` table, subclasses under `app/models/spree/payment_method_rules/`, preference-schema-driven config (the typed `preference :name, :array, of: :id, model:, scope:` idiom from `6.0-typed-preferences.md`), registry-validated `type`. This is the fourth instance of an established house pattern (PromotionRule, PriceRule, OrderRoutingRule) — deviating from it needs a reason.
- **No rules = available everywhere.** A payment method with zero rules behaves exactly as today. No backfill, no behavior change on upgrade.
- **AND semantics.** All active rules on a payment method must pass. No `match_policy` — "any" composition is a future addition if a real merchant needs it (none of the surveyed platforms offer OR at this layer). Rules never *choose between* records: if two records of one provider are both eligible for a cart, both are offered, so a merchant running two accounts keeps their market sets disjoint.
- **Empty preference within a rule = unrestricted** (`ChannelRule` with no channels selected passes) — matching the PriceRule/PromotionRule convention so admin half-configured rules fail open per-rule, not per-method.
- **One enforcement seam: `PaymentMethod#eligible_for_order?(purchase)`** = existing `available_for_order?(purchase)` gateway hook `&&` all active rules pass. `Spree::Purchase::PaymentProcessing#payment_methods` — shared by `Spree::Cart` and `Spree::Order` since the cart/order split — switches to it. The cart payments controller already checks `@cart.payment_methods.include?(payment_method)`, so payment creation is enforced by the same change. **Amended 2026-11-02:** the cart payment-sessions controller does *not* go through the seam today — it checks `available_for_order?` directly — so a storefront could open a session on an excluded method by id. Routing it through `payment_methods` is part of Phase 1, not a follow-up. (`collect_frontend_payment_methods`, named in earlier drafts, is a deprecated alias of `payment_methods` and is removed in 6.1.)
- **Admin/backoffice bypasses rules.** `Order#collect_backend_payment_methods` and the `Spree::Payment` model validation keep checking only `available_for_order?` + `available_for_store?`. An admin taking a phone order can use any active method — matching Shopify, where Payment Customization Functions run at checkout only. Rules are a *storefront checkout* concept.
- **Rules evaluate the purchase's persisted context** (`channel_id`, `market_id`, `total`, `currency`, `customer`, `company` — all columns on `spree_carts` and `spree_orders`), never `Spree::Current` — webhook/job/admin evaluation must not depend on request state.
- **Rules belong to the payment method only** — `belongs_to :payment_method`, store delegated (`payment_method.store`). No `store_id` column (unlike `OrderRoutingRule`, which needs it for its own scoping); id-preference `scope:` confines lookups to the owning store (`rule.store.channels`, `.markets`, `.customer_groups`, `.companies`) so cross-store ids can't sneak in.
- **Initial rule set: `ChannelRule`, `MarketRule`, `OrderTotalRule`, `CustomerGroupRule`, `CompanyRule`** (2026-11-02: `CompanyRule` promoted from "wait for the 6.1 Company tree" — the tree shipped 2026-08-25 and carts already name a company through `Spree::Purchase::Company`). A company rule matches the purchase's company node *or any of its ancestors*, the same walk address-book visibility does, so "Acme pays on terms" covers Acme's divisions. No Zone rule — Market is the geographic axis going forward (`6.0-tax-provider.md` drops Zone).
- **`OrderTotalRule` thresholds are per currency** (2026-11-02). `min_totals` and `max_totals` are `:hash` preferences keyed by currency with money values — the `Spree::Calculator::CurrencyAmounts` idiom — and a currency with no entry is unrestricted. A bare number applied in whatever currency the cart carries would make "5000" mean 5000 EUR and 5000 USD alike, which is wrong for every multi-market merchant and is exactly the case `MarketRule` exists for. Money is never a float (`6.0-money-correctness.md`).
- **Registry:** `Spree.payment_method_rules` (array of class names, defaulted in the engine, extensible by plugins) — same shape as `Spree.order_routing.rules`; `type_must_be_registered` validation on the base class.
- **Management is dashboard-only** (Admin API v3 + React dashboard). No legacy Rails admin UI, per the 2026-07-21 order-routing-rules decision — the legacy admin is in maintenance mode.
- **Multiple accounts of one provider are multiple `PaymentMethod` records, scoped by rules — Phase 2 of this plan** (2026-11-02, closing the 2026-07-23 grooming deferral). No credential map on one record (the Saleor shape): webhook endpoints, signing secrets, sessions and gateway customers are all keyed by record today, and a map would multiply every one of them by a key inside one row. No "account" model: a second record *is* the account, and `MarketRule` is its natural selector because a provider account settles in a currency and belongs to a legal entity, which is what a Market already expresses. Per-entity reporting is "group by payment method"; a legal-entity label lives in `metadata` until a merchant needs more. Consequences: the admin `types` endpoint's `installed` flag becomes a count and stops gating; the Store API payment method serializer gains `public_preferences`; the Stripe gateway declares its publishable key public; the Next.js storefront reads the key from the chosen method, with the environment variable as a fallback for one release.
- **Marketplaces: one Connect gateway per store in the first release** (2026-11-02). `SpreeStripe::PayoutProvider` resolves the gateway from the payment that funded the order, never from the store, so a transfer is always signed by the account that holds the charge. While a store's payout provider is Stripe Connect, only one Stripe payment method may be active — a validation on the gateway, with a plain error naming the other record. A seller's connected account is keyed by provider (`reference_system = 'stripe'`) and belongs to one platform account; keying it per platform account, with onboarding per account and balances grouped by account, is deferred until a marketplace actually runs two accounts (Open Questions).
- **Admin API follows the delivery-method-rules shape** (2026-11-04). The admin payment method always embeds its rules as `rules` and accepts the same `rules` array on create and update (the `Spree::TypedAssociations` writer), so the dashboard saves the method and its conditions in one request. Nested CRUD lives entirely under the method (`/payment_methods/:id/rules[/:rule_id]`) and discovery at `/payment_method_rules/types`. This replaces the shallow `PATCH|DELETE /payment_method_rules/:id` and `expand=payment_method_rules` drafted earlier: the newer sibling family (`DeliveryMethodRule`) is the house shape, and a field read as `rules` must be writable as `rules`.
- **`CompanyRule` has a presence mode** (2026-11-04). Beside `company_ids` (matched against the purchase's company or any ancestor) it carries `company_orders_only`, default true: with no companies picked, the rule still requires the purchase to be placed for a company, which expresses "invoice for every company buyer" without a customer group. Off, and with no companies, it restricts nothing. Same preference name and meaning as `DeliveryMethodRules::CompanyRule`.
- **Both phases ship in one pull request, with a companion storefront pull request** (2026-11-04). Phase 2 has no migration and the plan targets both for 6.1. The `spree/storefront` change (publishable key from the method, environment-variable fallback) is a separate pull request on that repository, linked from the main one.
- **Net-terms is out of scope.** The wholesale demo is served by an offline method (`Check` today, `PaymentMethod::BankTransfer` when `6.0-6.1-b2b-payment-terms.md` ships it) plus a `ChannelRule` or `CompanyRule`. Rules decide *which methods a buyer may choose*; the payment terms plan owns *what is due when* through the `payment_terms` snapshot on the purchase. The two compose and neither replaces the other.
- **`storefront_visible` is orthogonal and untouched.** Rules filter *within* the storefront-visible set.

## Design Details

### Base class

```ruby
# app/models/spree/payment_method_rule.rb
module Spree
  class PaymentMethodRule < Spree.base_class
    has_prefix_id :pmrule

    belongs_to :payment_method, class_name: 'Spree::PaymentMethod',
               inverse_of: :payment_method_rules, touch: true

    delegate :store, to: :payment_method

    attribute :active, :boolean, default: true

    validates :type, :payment_method, presence: true
    # One instance of each rule kind per payment method — duplicates would
    # either be a no-op or contradict themselves under AND semantics.
    validates :type, uniqueness: { scope: [:payment_method_id, *spree_base_uniqueness_scope] }
    validate :type_must_be_registered

    scope :active, -> { where(active: true) }

    # @param purchase [Spree::Cart, Spree::Order]
    # @return [Boolean] whether the payment method may be offered for this purchase
    def eligible?(purchase)
      raise NotImplementedError, "#{self.class.name} must implement #eligible?"
    end

    def self.human_name
      Spree.t("payment_method_rule_types.#{api_type}.name", default: api_type.titleize)
    end

    def self.human_description
      Spree.t("payment_method_rule_types.#{api_type}.description", default: '')
    end

    registers_subclasses_via { Array(Spree.payment_method_rules) }
  end
end
```

Table: `spree_payment_method_rules` — `type` (string, null: false), `payment_method_id` (reference, null: false), `active` (boolean, null: false), `preferences` (text), timestamps. Index on `payment_method_id`; unique index on `(payment_method_id, type)`.

### Rules

```ruby
# app/models/spree/payment_method_rules/channel_rule.rb
module Spree
  module PaymentMethodRules
    class ChannelRule < Spree::PaymentMethodRule
      preference :channel_ids, :array, of: :id, model: 'Spree::Channel', default: [],
                 scope: ->(rule) { rule.store.channels }

      def eligible?(purchase)
        return true if preferred_channel_ids.empty?

        preferred_channel_ids.map(&:to_s).include?(purchase.channel_id.to_s)
      end
    end
  end
end
```

`MarketRule` is identical over `market_ids` / `purchase.market_id`. `CustomerGroupRule` matches when `purchase.customer` belongs to any preferred group (guest purchases fail a configured group rule — an invoice method restricted to "Wholesale" must never surface to guests). `CompanyRule` matches when `purchase.company` or any of its ancestors is in `company_ids` (a purchase with no company fails a configured company rule). `OrderTotalRule`:

```ruby
# app/models/spree/payment_method_rules/order_total_rule.rb
module Spree
  module PaymentMethodRules
    class OrderTotalRule < Spree::PaymentMethodRule
      # Per currency, as Calculator::CurrencyAmounts stores amounts. A currency
      # with no entry is unrestricted on that side.
      preference :min_totals, :hash, keys: :currency, values: :money, default: {}
      preference :max_totals, :hash, keys: :currency, values: :money, default: {}

      def eligible?(purchase)
        min = preferred_min_totals[purchase.currency]
        max = preferred_max_totals[purchase.currency]
        total = purchase.total.to_d
        (min.nil? || total >= min.to_d) && (max.nil? || total <= max.to_d)
      end
    end
  end
end
```

### Enforcement

```ruby
# Spree::PaymentMethod
has_many :payment_method_rules, class_name: 'Spree::PaymentMethodRule',
         inverse_of: :payment_method, dependent: :destroy

# Storefront checkout eligibility: the gateway hook AND every active rule.
def eligible_for_order?(purchase)
  available_for_order?(purchase) && payment_method_rules.active.all? { |rule| rule.eligible?(purchase) }
end
```

```ruby
# Spree::Purchase::PaymentProcessing#payment_methods (Cart and Order)
store.payment_methods.active.storefront_visible.includes(:payment_method_rules).select do |payment_method|
  !payment_method.store_credit? && payment_method.eligible_for_order?(self)
end
```

The cart payments controller already resolves through `@cart.payment_methods`. The cart payment-sessions controller switches from its direct `available_for_order?` check to the same `@cart.payment_methods.include?` test, so listing, payment creation and session creation are enforced by one seam and a storefront cannot attach an excluded method by id. `collect_backend_payment_methods`, the `Spree::Payment` validation and `available_for_store?` are unchanged.

### Admin API v3

Mirrors the order-routing-rules surface (nested CRUD + type discovery):

```
GET    /api/v3/admin/payment_methods/:payment_method_id/rules
POST   /api/v3/admin/payment_methods/:payment_method_id/rules
GET    /api/v3/admin/payment_methods/:payment_method_id/rules/:id
PATCH  /api/v3/admin/payment_methods/:payment_method_id/rules/:id
DELETE /api/v3/admin/payment_methods/:payment_method_id/rules/:id
GET    /api/v3/admin/payment_method_rules/types      # discovery: type, label, description, schema
```

- Serializer: `id`, `type`, `active`, `preferences` (prefixed ids in id-list preferences, decoded on write and re-encoded on read by the typed preference layer), timestamps.
- Scope gating: `read_payment_methods` / `write_payment_methods` (rules are part of the payment-method aggregate — no new scope).
- Admin payment-method serializer always embeds `rules`, and create/update accept the same `rules` array (2026-11-04, Key Decisions).
- `GET /api/v3/admin/payment_methods/types`: `installed` (boolean) is kept for one release, deprecated, beside a new `installed_count`. The create picker and the Integrations gallery stop filtering on it (Phase 2).

### Store API

Phase 1 adds no surface. The cart's payment-method listing is simply filtered; rules are never serialized to the storefront. (Same posture as channel gating: enforcement in the API, configuration invisible to clients.)

Phase 2 adds `public_preferences` to the shared `PaymentMethodSerializer` — the values of the keys a gateway lists in `public_preference_keys`, a hook that exists on the model today with no caller. `SpreeStripe::Gateway` lists `publishable_key` and its preference type changes from `:password` to `:string`: a publishable key is public by design and must not be masked on the wire. Other gateways list nothing and serialize an empty object.

### SDK & Dashboard

- `@spree/admin-sdk`: `client.paymentMethods.rules.{list,create,update,delete}` + `client.paymentMethodRules.types()`; typelizer types. `@spree/sdk`: `PaymentMethod.public_preferences`.
- Dashboard: rules editor inside the payment-method edit form — same schema-driven `PreferencesForm` composition as the channel sheet's routing-rules editor (add-rule picker fed by the `types` endpoint, active toggles). Translation keys under `payment_method_rule_types.*` (Ruby) and `admin.paymentMethodRules.*` (dashboard locales, all languages).
- Phase 2: `filterPaymentMethodProviderTypes` stops dropping installed providers; the picker labels a provider the store already has ("Stripe · 1 installed") and the Integrations gallery lists every record of a provider under its card. The edit form keeps reading an installed provider's schema from the same list.
- Storefront (`spree/storefront`): Stripe.js is initialised with `payment_method.public_preferences.publishable_key` when present, falling back to `NEXT_PUBLIC_STRIPE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` for one release; the environment-variables doc marks the variable optional.

### Marketplace guard (Phase 2)

- `SpreeStripe::PayoutProvider#gateway_for(seller_transfer)` resolves the gateway through the order's completed payment, raising a `GatewayError` that names the order when it has none. The class-level `gateway_for(store)` stays for account creation and the onboarding link, which have no order yet.
- The single-account rule is enforced on both writes that can break it. `SpreeStripe::Gateway` validates that it is the only active Stripe method in the store while `store.payout_provider_class` is `SpreeStripe::PayoutProvider`, with a message telling the merchant which record to deactivate — that covers activating a second record. Switching the store to Stripe Connect saves no gateway, so `SpreeStripe::PayoutProvider.available_for_store?` (the hook that already exists and feeds the admin provider listing's `available` flag) answers false while two or more Stripe methods are active, and `Spree::Store` validates a changed `preferred_payout_provider` against `available_for_store?` — a generic core validation that knows nothing about Stripe. Stores that settle sellers through the record-only `System` provider are unaffected.

### Seeds / sample data

Extend the 5.6 wholesale demo: sample data attaches a `ChannelRule` (wholesale channel) to the seeded offline payment method and leaves Stripe/card methods unrestricted — a fresh install then demos differentiated checkout payment options per channel out of the box.

## Migration Path

Additive only:

1. **Phase 1 — rules.** Migration creates `spree_payment_method_rules`. No changes to existing tables, no backfill — zero-rule methods behave exactly as before. Ship models, enforcement (including the payment-sessions seam fix), Admin API, SDK and dashboard together; the feature is invisible until a rule is created. Sample-data changes last.
2. **Phase 2 — multiple provider accounts.** `public_preferences` on the Store API serializer and the Stripe publishable key reclassified; `installed_count` on the types endpoint with `installed` deprecated; dashboard picker and gallery; storefront key resolution with the environment fallback; payout provider resolution and the Connect validation. No migration. A store with one record per provider sees no change.
3. 6.2 removes the deprecated `installed` flag and the storefront's environment-variable fallback.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Don't add ad-hoc `channel_id`/`market_id` columns or join tables to `Spree::PaymentMethod`** — channel/market confinement is a rule.
- **New checkout code must resolve payment methods through `Spree::Purchase::PaymentProcessing#payment_methods`** (or `eligible_for_order?`), never by re-querying `store.payment_methods` and filtering manually — the single seam is what makes enforcement airtight. The payment-sessions controller is the known violation and is fixed in Phase 1.
- **Nothing may assume one payment method per provider per store** — not gateway gems, not `Spree::Integration`-style uniqueness, not dashboard code, not the storefront. A provider gem that needs a per-store singleton (Stripe Connect is the one known case) states it as a validation with a reason, as above.
- **Provider gems declare public configuration through `public_preference_keys`**, never by exposing a preference through a custom serializer or by asking the storefront to configure it twice.
- **Per-gateway `available_for_order?` overrides remain valid** (StoreCredit's coverage check, custom gateways) — rules layer on top, they don't replace the hook.
- **`6.0-integrations-admin.md`'s "one integration per (store, type)" stands.** Payment credentials never moved onto `Spree::Integration`; the multi-account decision here is about payment methods only.

## Open Questions

- **Seller onboarding per platform account.** A connected account belongs to one Stripe platform account, so a marketplace running two Stripe records needs each seller onboarded on each, with the external-id key, the onboarding screen, balances and sweeps all per account. Deferred until a marketplace actually needs two accounts; the Phase 2 validation keeps the single-account case correct meanwhile.
- **Backend enforcement toggle.** If merchants ask for rules to bind admin-created orders too, add an explicit store preference rather than changing the default. Unblocked by a merchant ask.
- **Overlap warning in the dashboard.** Two records of one provider whose rules both pass for some market show up together at checkout, which is correct AND behaviour but probably a misconfiguration. A warning when market sets overlap is polish for after Phase 2.
