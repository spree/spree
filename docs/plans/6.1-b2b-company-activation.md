# B2B Company Activation & the Company-Only Storefront

**Status:** In development — design finalized 2026-08-30, implementation started 2026-10-27
**Target:** Spree 6.1 (retargeted 2026-10-27: 6.0 shipped stable without these mechanisms. The 2026-08-30 pull-forward to 6.0 is spent — every piece is additive and needs no breaking window, so it lands in the next minor instead)
**Depends on:** B2B Companies & Catalogs (6.0-b2b-companies-and-catalogs.md), B2B Quantity Rules (6.0-b2b-quantity-rules.md — catalog terms), gated storefront access (shipped 5.6)
**Author:** Damian + Claude
**Last updated:** 2026-11-02 (storefront registration moved to Enterprise)
**Tracking:** (not yet opened)

## Summary

A merchant has no way to gate pricing on having a commercial relationship.
This plan adds the **open-source mechanisms**: a `company_required`
storefront access posture ("prices and checkout require an active company
standing"), a machine-readable `pricing_access` reason code beside hidden
prices, and a registered **company activation policy** consulted everywhere
a company's commercial standing matters. In OSS the default policy
activates every company, and companies are created by staff and joined
through invitations, so the posture means "only businesses the merchant has
set up" — a real trade-store gate on its own.

Storefront registration is not part of open source (decided 2026-11-02).
Businesses applying from the storefront, verification requirements per
country or region, the review queue and approval are one Enterprise
onboarding product built on these mechanisms.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **Storefront registration is Enterprise, not OSS** (2026-11-02,
  supersedes the 2026-08-30 split that put an instant-registration endpoint
  in OSS). Instant registration made the posture pointless — anyone with an
  email address could reach prices in two calls — and an OSS approve/reject
  step would be the half every merchant outgrows as soon as they need
  checks. Registration, requirements, review and approval ship together.
  OSS onboards buyers through staff-created companies and invitations, and
  a host app can build its own front door on the policy and the models.
- **`Spree::Companies::ActivationPolicy` is the single seam** (2026-08-30):
  a dependency-registered class
  (`Spree::Dependencies.company_activation_policy_class`, mirroring
  `storefront_access_policy_class`) answering `active?(company)` and
  `pricing_access_code(user:, store:)`. The OSS default answers active for
  every company. Every place that resolves a company for **visibility,
  pricing, or checkout** consults it: `Catalog.for_company`, both
  `sole_standing_company` implementations (`Purchase::Company` and
  `Products::ForContext` — mirrored, by existing constraint), the cart
  `company_id` write validation, and the `company_required` posture.
  Subtree semantics (a suspended parent covering its divisions) are the
  policy implementation's job, which is why the seam takes the node.
- **No status column on `spree_companies` in OSS.** The lifecycle
  (pending / review / approved / rejected / suspended, the requirements
  checklist, the queue) lives on an Enterprise-owned application record
  referencing the company — OSS tables never reference Enterprise models,
  and OSS behavior flows entirely through the policy. This supersedes the
  same-day draft that put five statuses on Company.
- **A fourth storefront access posture: `company_required`** (2026-08-30;
  renamed from `approval_required` 2026-11-01, before release — nothing is
  approved under the default policy, and `approval_required` is already the
  Enterprise order-approval checkout response)
  beside `public | prices_hidden | login_required`. Guests and
  authenticated buyers without a standing over a policy-active company
  browse the catalog with prices null and checkout refused; `prices_hidden`
  keeps its shipped guest-only semantics untouched. Under the default
  policy this reads as "prices for members of companies the merchant set
  up"; under the Enterprise policy it becomes "prices for approved
  companies only".
- **A reason code travels with hidden prices.** Serializers today null
  prices with no explanation. The gating concern computes `pricing_access`
  — `null` (visible) | `login_required` | `company_required` | codes the
  registered policy supplies (Enterprise adds `approval_pending`) —
  surfaced wherever prices are nulled, following the checkout-requirements
  `code:` convention; the channel serializer also exposes
  `storefront_access`. Storefronts render "Sign in" / "Awaiting approval"
  from the code, never by interpreting bare nulls.
- **Checkout is gated by a requirement, standing is not gated at all.** A
  new checkout requirement (`company_activation_required` code) refuses
  customer-initiated completion on `company_required` channels without a
  policy-active cart company — same shape as `guest_checkout_not_allowed`.
  Self-service standing (`standing_for?`, `company_standing`,
  `Storefront::AccessPolicy`) never consults the policy: members of an
  inactive company keep their company pages, address book, members and
  invitations — that is the pre-approval workspace the Enterprise flow
  needs.
- **Customer-group membership is never an activation mechanism** — groups
  price (catalog assignments), the policy activates. The seeded Wholesale
  group's "approval primitive" comment is retired with this plan.
- **`pricing_access` rides the priced payloads** (2026-10-27): the code is a
  request-level fact injected through `serializer_params` and rendered on
  product, variant, cart, order and line-item payloads (null when prices are
  visible) — not a channel-only attribute a storefront would have to
  correlate across responses. It is a Store API field only (2026-11-01):
  rendered when the request's gating params supply it, so event payloads
  omit it, and dropped from the admin, seller and email serializers. Its
  values are an open list (`PricingAccess` in `@spree/core`).
- **Receipts are never redacted** (2026-10-27): completed orders, order
  history and the customer's own balances opt out of the gate through a
  `renders_receipts!` macro — the posture protects catalog prices, not the
  record of money already taken. Guests cannot complete on
  `company_required` channels, so a guest's gate is authentication and the
  company requirement addresses signed-in buyers only.
- **An explicitly named inactive company keeps attribution** (2026-10-27):
  `resolved_company` returns the stamped node. What an inactive company
  loses is its agreements (`Catalog.groups_for_company`) and its exemptions
  (`Tax::ResolveExemptions`), never the sale's identity, address book or PO
  process. A placed order is exempt from the question entirely, the way its
  tax registration already is.
- **Two remediations, two codes** (2026-10-27): completion refuses with
  `company_selection_required` when the buyer's standing already covers an
  active company and they simply have not picked a node, and
  `company_activation_required` otherwise. One code for both would send a
  multi-company buyer to a "join or register your business" page.

## Design Details

### Activation policy

```ruby
class Spree::Companies::ActivationPolicy
  # Whether the company may act commercially (catalogs, pricing, checkout).
  def active?(_company) = true

  # The pricing_access code for a viewer lacking prices, or nil.
  def pricing_access_code(user:, store:)
    return 'login_required' if user.blank?
    'company_required' unless user.company_standing(store: store).any? { |c| active?(c) }
  end
end
```

Consulted (all existing call sites, no new resolution paths):
`Catalog.for_company` (assignments of active companies only), both
`sole_standing_company`s (memberships of active companies only),
`customer_has_standing_over_company` (cart write gains a distinct
`:not_active` error), the posture + checkout requirement. Completed orders
answer from their stamped `company_id`, untouched.

### Gating

`Channel::Gating::STOREFRONT_ACCESS += ['company_required']`; store-level
fallback validation updated. `hide_prices?` becomes posture-aware; the
`pricing_access` code rides `serializer_params` beside `hide_prices` and is
exposed on the payloads that null prices.

## Migration Path

1. `ActivationPolicy` + dependency registration + the resolution call-site
   consultations (behavior-neutral under the default policy — specs assert
   that explicitly; shared-concern battery runs against Cart AND Order).
2. `company_required` posture + `pricing_access` code + checkout
   requirement + serializer exposure.
3. Storefront reference: code-driven price labels.
4. Retire the Wholesale-group seed comment.

Nothing to migrate; every change is additive and inert until a merchant
selects the posture or registers a policy.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Any new code resolving a company for visibility, pricing, or checkout
  consults the activation policy** — and the two `sole_standing_company`
  implementations stay mirrored.
- **Never approval-gate self-service standing.**
- **New prices-hidden surfaces carry the `pricing_access` code** — no bare
  nulls.
- **No status/approval columns on `spree_companies`** — lifecycle state is
  the Enterprise application record's; OSS asks the policy.
- **Don't build interim approval primitives beside this mechanism** (customer-group
  membership, boolean flags); anything approval-shaped waits for this
  mechanism.
- **No storefront company registration endpoint in OSS** — it ships with
  Enterprise onboarding, together with its requirements and review.

## Open Questions

None for the OSS scope. Registration and everything after it belong to the
Enterprise plan.
