## Summary

Rebuild the B2B company model as a **multi-level organization tree** and pull
**Catalogs** forward into 6.0. The Company → CompanyLocation → CompanyContact
tree that shipped with the tax-provider work is merged but unreleased, so this
plan replaces it outright — no bridges, no data migration — with a single
self-referential `Spree::Company` node (typed `company` | `division`), an
address book per node, and a membership join that gives a buyer standing over
a subtree. Catalogs (assortment + optional PriceList, assignable to channels,
customer groups, markets, and company nodes with subtree semantics) move from
6.1 into 6.0 alongside it.

The strategic driver: no competitor ships organization hierarchy in open
source. Shopify's B2B is flat Company → Location; Medusa's starter is flat
Company → Employee; depth exists only in paid tiers elsewhere. Spree 6.0
ships it free, with the directory + hierarchy + catalogs in OSS and
governance (roles, approvals, spend limits, terms) layered on top by
Enterprise through hooks and policies — those plans live in the
`spree-enterprise-v2` repository, never here.

## Why the shipped model is being replaced

The merged tree copied Shopify's shape because the tax work needed only the
minimum. Shopify's `CompanyLocation` conflates three jobs — organizational
sub-unit, ship-to/bill-to address pair, and commercial-terms anchor — which is
its best-known weakness: one billing entity with forty ship-to sites means
forty locations and forty rounds of catalog/terms/exemption assignment. The
replacement unbundles those jobs (tree node / address book / membership) and
makes flat usage the trivial case: one node, several addresses, a few members
is exactly the old model's power with fewer moving parts. This is the last
moment the swap is free — nothing is released, so the old tables simply never
existed as far as any upgrade path is concerned.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

- **One structural entity, one table (2026-08-25).** `Spree::Company` is a
  self-referential tree (`parent_id`), store-scoped, depth capped at 5
  (constant, matching the commercetools/BigCommerce ceiling), cycle-checked
  on save. `Spree::CompanyLocation` and `Spree::CompanyContact` are deleted —
  models, tables, controllers, serializers, dashboard pages. `kind` starts as
  a plain string; **if the kinds ever grow diverging behavior, the sanctioned
  path is STI on this same table** (the standard `registers_subclasses_via`
  pattern) — never a separate `Division` model/table, which would force
  dual-FK or polymorphic references on every consumer.
- **Nodes are typed, and the type is load-bearing for tax.** `kind` is
  `company` (legal entity) or `division` (organizational unit). Tax
  identifiers and exemption certificates may exist **only on `company`
  nodes**; tax resolution for any node walks up to the **nearest
  self-or-ancestor `company` node** and stops there — a division *is* its
  legal entity, a subsidiary never borrows its parent's registration. Root
  nodes must be `kind: company` (a division with no legal-entity ancestor has
  no tax identity).
- **Membership is subtree-scoped, and in OSS every member can do everything
  (2026-08-25).** `Spree::CompanyMembership` joins a customer to a node;
  standing covers that node and everything below it. OSS ships **no company
  roles, no permission checks between members** — a member with standing may
  buy, see the subtree's purchases, and manage its addresses and members. All
  finer control (roles, capability checks, spend limits, approvals) enforces
  through injection points that are **no-ops in OSS**: the storefront access
  policy class (`Spree::Dependencies.storefront_access_policy_class`) and the
  checkout `validate` hooks. Enterprise fills them; OSS never grows guard
  logic of its own. The `role` string on membership is a cosmetic label only
  (the capability model is Enterprise, per `6.0-admin-rbac.md`).
- **People join by email, from either side (2026-08-25).** Staff (dashboard)
  and members (storefront) add people the same way: an email that matches a
  store customer becomes a membership immediately; an unknown email becomes
  a `Spree::CompanyInvitation` (plaintext token, 30-day expiry, functional
  invite email in `spree/emails`) whose acceptance runs `Customers::Create`
  and lands as a membership. Memberships themselves stay always-active and
  customer-backed — pending state lives on the invitation, never as a
  nullable `customer_id` or a status column on the membership.
- **Addresses are an address book, not structure.** `Spree::CompanyAddress`
  joins a node to owned `Spree::Address` rows with `label` +
  `default_billing`/`default_shipping` flags. How many ship-to sites a node
  has never dictates how many nodes exist.
- **Carts and orders point at a node.** `company_id` (any node — buying *for*
  a division is pointing at it), frozen at completion like every other order
  attribute. The bill-to/ship-to on the purchase are its normal address
  fields, conveniently filled from the node's address book. There is no
  `company_location_id` anywhere.
- **An empty assortment is a pricing-only overlay (2026-08-25, post-ship
  amendment).** A catalog with no curated products restricts nothing: its
  price list applies and the audience keeps seeing the whole channel range —
  which is how "everyone sees the public store, this company just gets
  special prices" is expressed (Shopify reaches the same outcome via
  catalogs defaulting to all-products). Any effective catalog with an empty
  assortment lifts the restriction for that buyer entirely. Consequently the
  price-list → assortment import is an **explicit action**
  (`import_products_from_price_list`, `POST /admin/catalogs/:id/import_products`,
  a dashboard button), never automatic — turning a catalog restrictive is a
  deliberate curation act.
- **Catalogs ship in 6.0** with the shape the 6.1 plan settled — `Catalog`
  (assortment + optional PriceList), `CatalogProduct`, polymorphic
  `CatalogAssignment` — except the assignable set is **CustomerGroup and
  Company** (the node replaces CompanyLocation). **Amended 2026-08-28:**
  the set originally included Channel and Market, but nothing ever read
  those rows — resolution and pricing consult only company-subtree and
  customer-group assignments plus the channel's `default_catalog_id` — so
  they were dropped pre-release rather than shipped as write-only traps.
  Assignments are buyer audiences; a channel's catalog is its
  `default_catalog_id` (the fallback shape a sales surface wants, which
  buyer catalogs override); a market catalog returns only together with a
  designed regional-assortment reader (see decisions.md 2026-08-28). A
  catalog assigned to a company node applies to its **subtree**: effective
  catalogs for a purchase node are the union of assignments on
  `self_and_ancestors`. Visibility is the union across effective catalogs;
  pricing takes the **nearest node's** assignment first, then catalog
  position.
- **No `tax_exempt` boolean anywhere** (reaffirming decisions.md 2026-08-07
  — exemption is certificates + provider resolution, never a flag).
- **No new state machines, no workflows for plain CRUD.** Company,
  membership, address, and catalog writes are ordinary ResourceController
  CRUD. Certificate verify/revoke keeps its existing workflow.
- **Enterprise governance layers on, never in.** Spending limits, approvals,
  company roles, payment terms, quotes: their plans live in
  `spree-enterprise-v2/docs/plans/`, and they consume OSS hooks
  (`carts.complete.validate`), the storefront access policy class, dashboard
  slots, and events. Any mechanism they need that does not exist is an OSS
  contribution first.
- **Unreleased migrations are rewritten in place.** The `2026080700000x`
  company migrations are edited to the new schema rather than stacked on —
  6.0 has not shipped, so no installation has run them. Worktree template
  databases must be rebuilt (`pnpm wt:template`).

## Design Details

### Spree::Company — the tree node

```ruby
class Spree::Company < Spree.base_class
  has_prefix_id :comp

  include Spree::SingleStoreResource
  include Spree::HasCustomFields
  include Spree::Metadata

  publishes_lifecycle_events

  KINDS = %w[company division].freeze
  MAX_DEPTH = 5

  belongs_to :store, class_name: 'Spree::Store'
  belongs_to :parent, class_name: 'Spree::Company', optional: true, inverse_of: :children
  has_many :children, class_name: 'Spree::Company', foreign_key: :parent_id,
           inverse_of: :parent, dependent: :destroy

  has_many :company_addresses, class_name: 'Spree::CompanyAddress', dependent: :destroy
  has_many :addresses, through: :company_addresses, class_name: 'Spree::Address'
  has_many :memberships, class_name: 'Spree::CompanyMembership', dependent: :destroy
  has_many :customers, through: :memberships, class_name: Spree.user_class.to_s

  has_many :tax_identifiers, class_name: 'Spree::TaxIdentifier', dependent: :destroy
  has_many :tax_exemption_certificates, class_name: 'Spree::TaxExemptionCertificate',
           dependent: :destroy

  has_many :catalog_assignments, as: :assignable, class_name: 'Spree::CatalogAssignment',
           dependent: :destroy
  has_many :catalogs, through: :catalog_assignments

  validates :name, presence: true
  validates :kind, inclusion: { in: KINDS }
  validates :external_id, uniqueness: { scope: :store_id }, allow_nil: true
  validate :parent_in_same_store
  validate :root_must_be_legal_entity      # kind == 'company' when parent_id.nil?
  validate :depth_within_cap               # ancestors walk, bounded by MAX_DEPTH
  validate :no_ancestry_cycle
end
```

Tree reads (all bounded by `MAX_DEPTH`, so ancestor walks are a loop over
`parent`, and subtree reads are a recursive CTE scope that works on
PostgreSQL, MySQL 8, and SQLite):

- `#ancestors` / `#self_and_ancestors` — leaf → root order
- `#self_and_descendants` — recursive CTE, used by subtree standing checks,
  catalog assignment resolution, and the dashboard tree
- `#root`
- `#legal_entity` — nearest self-or-ancestor with `kind == 'company'`. **The
  single tax anchor**: identifiers and certificates are always read through
  it, and the walk stops at the first `company` node whether or not it holds
  anything (a subsidiary with no VAT id has no VAT id — it does not borrow
  its parent's).

`kind` has no database default (per the string-column rule); the model
normalizes a blank kind to `company` on initialize. Divisions cannot own tax
identifiers or exemption certificates — model validation plus a 422 from the
nested Admin API endpoints. This constraint is deliberately a validation, not
a schema difference, so a future "division with its own foreign VAT
registration" (Shopify's per-location `taxRegistrationId` precedent) can be
allowed later by relaxing the validation alone — resolution already reads
through `legal_entity`, which would then simply become
nearest-holder-or-legal-entity.

### Spree::CompanyAddress — the address book

```ruby
class Spree::CompanyAddress < Spree.base_class
  has_prefix_id :caddr

  belongs_to :company, class_name: 'Spree::Company', inverse_of: :company_addresses
  belongs_to :address, class_name: 'Spree::Address', dependent: :destroy

  # label: 'Headquarters', 'Plant 2 dock', …
  # default_billing / default_shipping: at most one of each per node,
  # enforced the same way Channel#default is (after_save demotion + partial
  # unique index on PG/SQLite, model validation on MySQL).
end
```

The address rows are owned outright (mirroring what `CompanyLocation` did) —
never shared with a customer's address book. A node with no defaults falls
back to its nearest ancestor's defaults when the dashboard or Store API
prefills a checkout, but nothing is ever resolved implicitly at order time:
the order's own address columns are the truth, as today.

### Spree::CompanyMembership — people

```ruby
class Spree::CompanyMembership < Spree.base_class
  has_prefix_id :cmem

  belongs_to :company, class_name: 'Spree::Company', inverse_of: :memberships
  belongs_to :customer, class_name: Spree.customer_class.to_s

  validates :customer_id, uniqueness: { scope: :company_id }
  # role: cosmetic label only ('buyer' by convention). OSS has no company
  # permission model — every member can do everything within their standing;
  # the capability model is Enterprise
  # (spree-enterprise-v2/docs/plans/b2b-companies.md).
end
```

**Standing** — a membership at node N authorizes the customer to act for N
and every descendant of N. `Customer#company_standing(store:)` returns the
set of nodes the customer can purchase for within a store (memberships on
that store's trees, expanded by subtree). Customers stay global; companies
are store-scoped, so standing is always evaluated per store.

### Spree::CompanyInvitation — bringing in people who have no account yet

Memberships stay always-active and always customer-backed; the
not-yet-registered case gets its own small model instead of a nullable
`customer_id` + status on the membership (mirroring the existing staff
invitation shape):

```ruby
class Spree::CompanyInvitation < Spree.base_class
  has_prefix_id :cinv

  include Spree::Metadata   # carries extension payloads (e.g. an Enterprise role reference)

  belongs_to :company, class_name: 'Spree::Company', inverse_of: :invitations
  belongs_to :inviter, class_name: Spree.customer_class.to_s, optional: true  # nil when staff invites

  has_secure_token :token   # plaintext, like the staff invitation + setup tokens

  normalizes :email, with: ->(email) { email.strip.downcase }

  validates :email, presence: true
  validates :email, uniqueness: { scope: :company_id, conditions: -> { pending } }
  # expires_at set on create (EXPIRY = 30.days); accepted_at / revoked_at stamps
end
```

Flow (both entry points converge here):

1. `POST …/members` with a `customer_email` that matches no customer in the
   store → invitation row + `company_invitation.created` event; the
   invitation email (with the token link) ships in `spree/emails` — it is
   functional mail like the staff invitation, not a marketing welcome, so
   the no-welcome-email rule (`Customers::Create`) is untouched. Headless
   storefronts own the acceptance page; the link target resolves like other
   customer-facing links (store URL), and the event lets a host app send its
   own mail instead.
2. Acceptance is unauthenticated by token: `GET …/:token` shows what is
   being joined; `POST …/:token/accept` either takes a registration payload
   — running `Customers::Create`, the single customer-creation workflow — or
   an authenticated request from a customer whose email meanwhile registered
   (the invitation binds to that customer). Either way the membership is
   created, `accepted_at` stamped, token spent.
3. Any member with standing (or staff) can revoke a pending invitation;
   expired/revoked tokens 404 on lookup. Re-inviting after expiry/revocation
   creates a fresh row — the pending-uniqueness scope permits it.

No roles on the invitation — it grants exactly what a direct membership
grants. Enterprise attaches its role payload through the same dialog slot,
stored on the invitation's `metadata` (or its own table referencing the
row), and applies it when acceptance creates the membership via the
`company_invitation.accepted` event.

### Carts and orders

`spree_carts.company_id` and `spree_orders.company_id` replace
`company_location_id`. `Spree::Purchase::Company` (shared Cart/Order concern)
becomes:

- `#company` — the explicit node, no heuristics on completed orders (frozen
  column wins, as today).
- `#b2b?` — `company_id.present?`.
- Resolution for in-flight carts: explicit `company_id` if set (validated —
  the cart's customer must have standing over that node, and the node must
  belong to the cart's store); otherwise **sole standing**: a customer whose
  standing in the store resolves to exactly one membership uses that
  membership's node; a customer with several memberships resolves to
  nothing until the cart says which node it is for. This replaces (and
  finally settles) the "provisional — revisit" heuristic in the shipped
  concern: ambiguity is resolved by an explicit cart attribute, not by
  guessing.
- Tax reads all go through `company&.legal_entity` — `ResolveExemptions`
  reads the legal entity's certificates `for_address`, the identifier
  resolution chain reads the legal entity's identifiers above the customer's
  own row (unchanged precedence, new anchor).

### Catalogs (pulled forward from the 6.1 plan)

`Spree::Catalog`, `Spree::CatalogProduct`, and `Spree::CatalogAssignment`
ship as the 6.1 plan specified (store-scoped catalog, product join,
polymorphic assignment, `spree_channels.default_catalog_id`) with these
amendments:

- **Assignable types: CustomerGroup, Company** (narrowed from
  Channel/CustomerGroup/Market/Company on 2026-08-28 — the dropped two were
  write-only; see the amendment in Key Decisions). `CompanyLocation` is
  gone; a company-node assignment applies to the node's subtree.

- **The product join carries no position.** A catalog decides *what* a buyer
  sees, never the order they see it in — the storefront resolver uses
  `CatalogProduct` purely as a membership filter, and the listing keeps
  whatever sort the shopper chose. The column, `acts_as_list`, the
  `reposition` endpoint and the dashboard's drag handle are all removed; the
  admin lists an assortment by name. (`Catalog#position` is unaffected — it
  still breaks ties when several catalogs apply to one buyer.) Category and
  collection membership stays positioned, because those *do* drive storefront
  display order.
- **Effective catalogs for a purchase node** = union of assignments on
  `self_and_ancestors`. The parent assigns the group-wide catalog once; a
  branch adds its own extra catalog without re-assigning the shared one.
- **Visibility** (`Spree::Products::ForContext.call(store:, channel:,
  customer:, company: nil)`): base set is the channel's publications; if the
  buyer resolves to a company node with effective catalogs, intersect with
  their union; else if the customer's group has a catalog, intersect with
  that; else all channel listings (channel default catalog if set). The
  gated-access layer (5.6) still runs first — a `login_required` guest never
  reaches this resolver. **Reaffirmed 2026-09-04 (V-3570):** those are
  alternatives, not layers — a buyer whose company subtree carries any
  catalog is never also served their customer group's, and trade tiers over
  a company tree are expressed as company assignments (see decisions.md).
- **Pricing**: `Pricing::Resolver` checks the effective catalogs'
  PriceLists in **nearest-node-first** order (the purchase node's own
  assignments before its parent's, catalog `position` breaking ties within a
  node), then the existing PriceRule matching, then base price.
- **No Company ↔ CustomerGroup link.** The 6.1 sketch's
  `spree_companies.customer_group_id` is dropped from the design: catalog
  assignment targets the company node directly, so the link's only remaining
  use would have been group-targeted promotions/price rules — and a company
  "being in" a group does not put its buyers in that group, so the link
  promised composition it could not deliver. Group-style targeting of
  companies (promotion rules, price rules) can arrive later as explicit
  `CompanyRule` kinds on those STI families.

### Admin API v3

```
# Companies — tree-aware CRUD

DELETE             /api/v3/admin/company_memberships/:id
GET                /api/v3/admin/companies/:company_id/invitations
DELETE             /api/v3/admin/company_invitations/:id      # revoke

# Unchanged surfaces (anchor moves to the node; 422 on division nodes)
…/companies/:company_id/tax_identifiers (+ :validate)
…/companies/:company_id/tax_exemption_certificates (+ verify/revoke/download)

# Catalogs — per the 6.1 plan
GET/POST/PATCH/DELETE /api/v3/admin/catalogs
POST   /api/v3/admin/catalogs/:id/products      # bulk add { product_ids: [...] }
DELETE /api/v3/admin/catalogs/:id/products/:product_id

DELETE /api/v3/admin/catalog_assignments/:id
```

The `/company_locations/*` and `/company_contacts/*` routes, controllers, and
serializers are deleted. Company serializer gains `parent_id`, `kind`,
`children_count`, `members_count`; permissions stay on the `customers`
resource for companies and gain nothing new for catalogs beyond the existing
`products` pair (a catalog is merchandising data).

### Store API — company self-service (every member, full power)

Resolved 2026-08-25: OSS ships full directory self-service on the Store API,
with **no role model** — any member with standing over a node can act on that
node and its subtree. Guards, limits, and role checks are Enterprise; OSS
exposes only the injection points.

```
GET    /api/v3/store/account/companies            # my memberships: node id/name/kind + ancestor path
GET    /api/v3/store/companies/:id                # node detail (requires standing)
PATCH  /api/v3/store/companies/:id                # name, custom fields
GET/POST      /api/v3/store/companies/:id/addresses
PATCH/DELETE  /api/v3/store/company_addresses/:id
GET/POST      /api/v3/store/companies/:id/members # POST takes customer_email — member or invitation, see below
DELETE        /api/v3/store/company_members/:id
GET/DELETE    /api/v3/store/companies/:id/invitations, /api/v3/store/company_invitations/:id
GET    /api/v3/store/companies/:id/orders         # completed purchases across the node's subtree
PATCH  /api/v3/store/cart                         # company_id writable (validated against standing)

# Unauthenticated invitation acceptance
GET    /api/v3/store/company_invitations/:token         # lookup: company name, store, invited email
POST   /api/v3/store/company_invitations/:token/accept  # registration payload OR authenticated accept
```

- **Authorization = standing + the access policy, never CanCanCan and never
  roles.** Every endpoint first requires a membership on the node or an
  ancestor, then consults `Spree::Storefront::AccessPolicy`
  (`readable?`/`writable?`/`scope`) — whose OSS default answers *yes* for any
  member. Enterprise registers its `CompanyAccessPolicy` subclass via
  `Spree::Dependencies.storefront_access_policy_class` to enforce
  capabilities, and its checkout guards ride the existing
  `carts.complete.validate` hook. OSS controllers contain no conditional
  logic beyond the standing check and the policy call.
- **Adding a member takes an email and does the right thing** (resolved
  2026-08-25, folded in from the deferred list): if a customer with that
  email exists in the store, the membership is created immediately; if not,
  a `Spree::CompanyInvitation` is created and the invite email goes out.
  Any member can invite — same no-permissions rule as everything else, and
  the same dialog slot (`company_membership.form_fields`) lets Enterprise
  attach its role payload to either outcome.
- Cart/order Store serializers gain `company_id` + `company_name`.
  `Products::ForContext` and the pricing resolver read the cart's node (or
  sole standing), so a signed-in buyer browses their catalog with no extra
  calls; a multi-node buyer sets the cart's `company_id` explicitly.
- Tax identifiers and exemption certificates stay **admin-managed** in 6.0 —
  buyer-submitted certificates are a natural follow-on but need the
  verification queue UX to make sense first.
- Accepted trade-off, stated plainly: **within a company, OSS trusts every
  member.** A member can remove other members (including all of them) or
  redirect deliveries by editing the address book. That is the deliberate
  price of shipping self-service without a roles system; merchants who need
  restraint buy the governance layer, and the injection points mean no OSS
  schema changes when they do.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Never reference `company_location_id` or the location/contact models** in
  new code, specs, or docs — they are deleted, not deprecated (unreleased
  code gets no bridge, the recorded exception to the bridges convention).
- **Tax reads on a purchase go through `company&.legal_entity`**, never
  through `company` directly — a division holds no registrations and code
  that reads the node's own identifiers silently loses the exemption.
- **Standing checks are subtree checks.** Any authorization-ish question
  ("may this customer act for node N") is `memberships on
  self_and_ancestors(N)`, never an equality check on one node. The admin
  customer filter is the worked example: `Spree.customer_class`'s
  `with_standing_for_company` scope (2026-08-30, ransackable) matches members
  of the node OR any ancestor, and a plain `companies_id_in` predicate is
  WRONG here — it hides the group-level buyer who legitimately purchases for
  a subsidiary, and would offer a narrower set than the cart's own standing
  validation accepts.
- **No governance fields on OSS models.** Spending limits, approval settings,
  role capabilities, payment terms do not get columns here — their plans live
  in `spree-enterprise-v2/docs/plans/` and consume hooks/slots/policies (the
  model-not-the-feature rule, decisions.md 2026-08-07).
- **Catalog and company lookups are store-scoped** like every controller
  lookup (`current_store.companies`, `current_store.catalogs`) including
  incidental resolutions (`parent_id`, `assignable_id`, `price_list_id`).
- **`kind` values are the fixed two.** New node semantics (franchise, cost
  centre labels, …) are custom fields or future additive kinds — never
  overloaded onto `division`. Behavioral divergence between kinds means STI
  on the same table, never a second model.
- **Storefront company endpoints authorize by standing + the access policy,
  never by role.** No `if role == 'admin'` (or any member-vs-member
  permission logic) ever appears in OSS controllers, models, or the
  dashboard — that logic belongs to the Enterprise policy subclass and hook
  handlers. OSS code adds injection points, not guards.
