# Seller Product Submission and Review

**Status:** Implemented (2026-08-26, PR #14497) — `proposed`/`rejected` added through `add_status`, the `Products::Propose`/`Approve`/`Reject` workflows live beside the existing trio, `Spree::ProductSubmission` records each decision on a row the seller cannot reach, the permission catalog gained `product_types` and `publishing`, the seller branch moves status only through member actions (`PATCH /seller/products/:id/submit|draft|archive`, `status` dropped from permitted params), and the operator branch gained `PATCH /admin/products/:id/approve|reject` plus the review buttons. **Remaining:** lifting the rest of the product form cards into `@spree/dashboard-ui` — only `status-card.tsx` moved, so the seller panel still renders its own product page rather than the shared cards
**Target:** Spree 6.0
**Depends on:** `6.0-multi-vendor-marketplace.md`, `6.0-seller-onboarding-requirements.md`, `6.0-admin-rbac.md`, `6.0-product-types.md`
**Author:** Damian Legawiec
**Last updated:** 2026-08-27

## Summary

A seller can list a product but must not publish one. Today they can: the
seller products endpoint permits `status` as a plain attribute, so a seller
sends `status: 'active'` and their product is live in the marketplace catalog
with nobody having looked at it. This plan closes that by giving products a
review lifecycle — the seller submits, the operator decides — and by splitting
the two capabilities that currently ride the `products` permission key and
should not: managing product types, and publishing to sales channels.

It also gives the seller panel the same product form the operator's dashboard
has, rather than the four-field placeholder it ships today. The form cards move
into `@spree/dashboard-ui` so both dashboards render one definition and cannot
drift.

## Key Decisions (do not deviate without discussion)

1. **Two new statuses, `proposed` and `rejected`**, added through
   `add_status` — additive by design, so core workflows guarding on core
   statuses keep passing. We keep our own vocabulary: `active` is what the
   marketplace reference calls `published`, and renaming it would churn every
   existing consumer for no gain. `archived` is untouched.

2. **The lifecycle is workflows, never a state machine.** `Products::Propose`,
   `Products::Approve` and `Products::Reject` join the existing `Activate` /
   `Archive` / `Draft` trio, each with a leading `validate` hook. There is no
   transition graph — that would be a state machine by another name (see
   `6.0-service-workflows.md`).

3. **`status` stops being a writable attribute on the seller branch.** A
   seller moves a product with an explicit member action, never by sending a
   status in an update payload. The operator's branch keeps the plain
   attribute for the operator's own catalog — but not for a product in
   review (revised 2026-08-26): a direct status write or `bulk_status_update`
   on a `proposed` or `rejected` product refuses, because leaving review is
   a decision, and a decision that skips `Approve`/`Reject` records no
   reviewer and closes no submission row. A bulk approve, if ever needed, is a
   loop over the workflow, never `update_all`.

4. **A seller may return an `active` product to `draft` or `archived`, but may
   never reach `active` themselves.** Taking your own listing down is not a
   review decision; putting it up is. This mirrors the legacy gem, which
   granted `activate` only for products already `active`.

5. **`product_types` becomes its own catalog resource, read-only for the
   seller audience.** A seller fills in a type's custom fields; they do not
   define types. Per `6.0-admin-rbac.md`, a new sensitive capability gets its
   own catalog entry rather than riding an existing key.

6. **`ProductPublication` moves off the `products` key onto a publishing
   resource with no seller audience.** Which channels carry a product is
   marketplace merchandising, not a seller's to set.

7. **A seller files a product into the marketplace's groupings; they do not
   extend them.** `category_ids` and `collection_ids` are writable — filing a
   product is part of maintaining it, which is why the operator's own endpoint
   permits them under `write_products` rather than `write_collections`. The
   categories and collections themselves are read-only pickers.

   **Tags are not writable at all.** They are tenanted to the store
   (`acts_as_taggable_tenant :store_id`), so a seller typing a new one adds a
   term to the marketplace's shared vocabulary rather than labelling their own
   product. Verified against both marketplace platforms we looked at: one
   exposes categories, collections, tags, types and attributes to sellers as
   read-only, with a single endpoint for placing a seller's own products into
   an existing category; the other's category tree and attribute schema are
   operator-only imports that sellers map onto. The legacy
   `spree_multi_vendor` gem took the same line (`cannot :manage_tags`,
   taxonomy `index`/`show` only).

8. **Custom field *values* are seller-writable; *definitions* are not.**
   `Spree::CustomField` stays on `products`; `Spree::MetafieldDefinition`
   is operator-only, and the seller panel renders the definitions it is given
   without the "add a definition" affordance.

9. **The product form cards live in `@spree/dashboard-ui`**, headless and
   generic over the record type — data by props, no SDK import, no provider
   import. `MediaCard`'s one `adminClient.media.list` call becomes an injected
   prop. This is what makes "the same as in dashboard" true by construction
   rather than by discipline.

10. **No JSON preview on the seller panel.** It is an opt-in prop on the page
   shell; the seller form omits it.

11. **The review decision is a row, not a note on the product** (revised
   2026-08-26). Each submission and each decision on it is a
   `Spree::ProductSubmission`: who submitted, who decided, when, and the
   seller-visible note. The row records the trail — it never replaces the
   product's status, which stays the operational truth, exactly as a
   seller's own status lives on the seller while
   `SellerRequirementSubmission` rows carry the history. The original
   design put the rejection reason in `product.metadata`, which failed on
   three counts, in descending order:
   the seller products endpoint permits `metadata: {}`, so an ordinary save
   by the seller replaced the hash and silently erased the operator's reason
   (proven over HTTP — the status stayed `rejected` with no recoverable
   reason); a second rejection overwrote the first, so nobody could see a
   seller sent back three times for the same thing; and there was nowhere to
   record who decided — `Approve` and `Reject` both already accept
   `reviewer:` and the admin controller already passes the current user,
   which both then discarded. The rejected rationale ("a table for one
   string") survives none of these: the row is not one string, it is the
   audit trail.

12. **The name is `ProductSubmission`, and `Spree::ProductReview` stays
   reserved for customer ratings.** Core's own `Spree.permissions`
   documentation uses `SpreeReviews::Review` under a `reviews` catalog
   resource as the canonical extension example, so taking the obvious name
   for an operator decision would displace it permanently. The row is named
   for the seller's act — the decision rides on it — exactly as
   `Spree::SellerRequirementSubmission` is, and the statuses read naturally
   against it: a *submission* is pending or withdrawn; a "withdrawn review"
   never was. Prefix `prodsub`, parallel to `selsub`.

## Design Details

### Status lifecycle

```text
draft ──propose──▶ proposed ──approve──▶ active
  ▲                    │
  │                    └──reject───▶ rejected
  │                                     │
  └──────────── seller revises ─────────┘

active ──▶ draft | archived        (seller may do this)
active ──▶ proposed                (never — approval is not re-requested)
```

- `draft` — the seller is still working. Not visible in the storefront.
- `proposed` — submitted, awaiting the operator. Not visible in the storefront.
- `active` — approved and on sale. Unchanged meaning.
- `rejected` — the operator turned it down. Not visible in the storefront. The
  seller edits and submits again, which returns it to `proposed`.
- `archived` — withdrawn. Unchanged meaning.

Only `active` is storefront-visible, which is already how `ProductScopes`
works — the new statuses inherit that for free precisely because they are not
`active`.

**Auto-approval.** A store preference (`auto_approve_seller_products`)
chains `Propose` straight into `Approve`, matching how
`auto_approve_sellers` works in `6.0-seller-onboarding-requirements.md`. A
marketplace that does not want to review every listing sets it once.

**Rejection reason.** `Reject` takes a `reason:` and records it on the
submission row, so the seller sees why. (It lived briefly in
`product.metadata`, which the seller could overwrite — Key Decision 11
records why that changed.)

### The submission record

```ruby
class Spree::ProductSubmission < Spree.base_class
  belongs_to :product
  belongs_to :submitted_by, class_name: Spree.admin_user_class.to_s, optional: true
  belongs_to :reviewed_by,  class_name: Spree.admin_user_class.to_s, optional: true

  has_status :pending, :approved, :rejected, :withdrawn, default: :pending
end
```

Columns: `product_id`, `status`, `submitted_by_id`, `reviewed_by_id`,
`reviewed_at`, `review_note` (text — the operator's, seller-visible; named
as on `SellerRequirementSubmission`, leaving `note` free for a future
seller-side message), `metadata`, timestamps. No `store_id`: the store is
derived through the product, exactly as `SellerRequirementSubmission`
derives it through the seller. Index
`(product_id, created_at)` — the latest row for a product is the one that
counts, the ones before it are how the marketplace got there.

**Append-only, and every exit from review closes the open row.** `Propose`
opens a `pending` row stamping `submitted_by`; `Approve` and `Reject` close
it with `reviewed_by`/`reviewed_at`; and a seller withdrawing a submission —
`Draft` or `Archive` on a `proposed` product — closes it `withdrawn`, so a
pending row always means "awaiting a decision", never "abandoned". A product
submitted, rejected, revised and resubmitted leaves the full sequence.
Approvals are recorded as deliberately as rejections: "when did this go on
sale, and who allowed it" is the question with the longest tail.

**Auto-approval says so.** The `auto_approve_seller_products` chain approves
with no human in the loop, and its row records
`metadata: { auto_approved: true }` — an approved row with a blank reviewer
must mean "this store does not review listings", never "someone approved
from the console and we lost their name".

**One note field, seller-visible.** An operator-only note is a real want (an
approver recording why they allowed something borderline) but has no caller
yet, and a second column is additive later — as is `note` for the seller's
own message, per the `SellerRequirementSubmission` layout. The reason
stays free text in 6.0 — a controlled reason vocabulary is the mature
shape, and it hangs off this row whenever a real need arrives.

### Core

- `Spree::Product`: `add_status :proposed, after: :draft` and
  `add_status :rejected, after: :proposed`. `STATUSES` stays as-is; the
  class attribute is what `has_status` validates against.
- New scopes fall out of `has_status` automatically (`.proposed`, `.rejected`).
- `Spree::Products::Propose`, `::Approve`, `::Reject` — workflows on the
  `Activate` template, each publishing an event (`product.proposed`,
  `product.approved`, `product.rejected`).
- Registered as `Spree.product_propose_workflow` and friends in
  `Spree::Dependencies`.
- `Spree::ProductSubmission` + the row-writing steps in `Propose`,
  `Approve`, `Reject`, `Draft` and `Archive` (the last two only when leaving
  `proposed`).

### API

Seller branch:

- `PATCH /seller/products/:id/submit` → `Products::Propose`
- `PATCH /seller/products/:id/draft` → `Products::Draft` (take down)
- `PATCH /seller/products/:id/archive` → `Products::Archive`
- `status` removed from `permitted_params`.
- `GET /seller/product_types` — read-only, for the type picker.
- `GET /seller/custom_field_definitions` — read-only, so the form can render
  the fields a seller fills in.

Admin branch:

- `PATCH /admin/products/:id/approve` → `Products::Approve`
- `PATCH /admin/products/:id/reject` → `Products::Reject` (takes `reason`)
- A `proposed` filter on the products index so an operator can find the queue.
- The admin product serializer embeds the latest submission
  (`submission`: status, review note, reviewer, timestamps); a full-trail
  endpoint waits for a real need. The seller serializer exposes the latest
  `review_note` only — never the reviewer's identity.

### Permissions

```ruby
register_resource(:product_types, group: :catalog, write: false,
                                  audiences: %i[seller],
                                  subjects: -> { [Spree::ProductType] })

register_resource(:publishing, group: :catalog,
                               subjects: -> { [Spree::ProductPublication] })
```

`Spree::ProductType` and `Spree::ProductPublication` leave the `products`
subject list. Approving and rejecting are `write_products` on the admin
branch — an operator who may edit the catalog may decide what enters it.

**Upgrade note.** The seeded `admin` role is unaffected: it holds no explicit
keys and takes full access, so it picks up new catalog resources
automatically. A *custom* role that was granted `write_products` does not
inherit `write_product_types` or `write_publishing` — splitting a capability
means the narrower grants have to be re-issued, which is the operator's call
and not something an upgrade should decide for them. Per
`6.0-admin-rbac.md`, roles are pure data: no code path rewrites a store's
saved permission arrays.

### Dashboard packages

`@spree/dashboard-ui` gains a `products/` directory holding the form cards,
each headless: no `adminClient`, no `useStore`, no locale import. The two apps
supply data, mutations and translated labels by prop.

The seller panel composes: General, Media, Prices, Variants, Inventory, SEO,
Categorization, Custom Fields, Metadata, Status (restricted), Translations.
It omits Publishing (channels), Tax, and the JSON preview.

`StatusCard` takes the allowed transitions as a prop rather than deriving
them, so the seller panel offers Submit / Take down / Archive while the
operator's offers the full set.

## Migration Path

1. Core statuses + the three workflows + dependency registration.
2. Permission catalog split (`product_types`, `publishing`), with the
   subjects moved off `products`.
3. Seller API: member actions, `status` dropped from permitted params,
   read-only product types and custom field definitions.
4. Admin API: approve/reject + the review queue filter.
5. Lift the form cards into `@spree/dashboard-ui`; repoint the operator's
   dashboard at them (no visual change — the same components).
6. Build the seller product form on the shared cards.
7. Data: nothing to migrate. Existing products keep their status; no row is
   `proposed` or `rejected` until somebody submits one.
8. The submission record (**implemented 2026-08-26**, on the same unreleased
   branch): `Spree::ProductSubmission` + migration,
   `Spree::ProductSubmissions::Close` as the single settling path, row writes
   in the five workflows, review-status refusal in `Products::Update` (so
   every caller inherits it) and skip-with-a-count in `bulk_status_update`,
   `Product#rejection_reason`/`#latest_submission`, admin + seller
   submission serializers embedded on the product as `submission`. No data
   task and no bridge — the metadata design never shipped, so it is
   rewritten in place and the `rejection_reason` key never reaches a
   release.

## Constraints on Current Work

- **Never permit `status` as a writable attribute on the seller branch.** A
  new seller endpoint touching products must move status through a workflow.
- **Do not add `Spree::ProductType` or `Spree::ProductPublication` back to
  the `products` permission subjects.** They are their own resources now.
- **A new product-form card goes into `@spree/dashboard-ui`**, not into
  `@spree/dashboard` — otherwise the two forms drift again, which is the
  thing this plan exists to prevent. It must take its data by prop and import
  no SDK.
- **Storefront visibility keys off `active` only.** A new status must not be
  added to the published scope without a deliberate decision.
- No `Product#submitted?`-style helper methods beyond what `has_status`
  generates, and no columns recording review state on the product — the
  product's status is its state, and the review's history is the row.
- **Never store an operator decision where the subject of the decision can
  write.** `metadata` on a seller-owned record is seller-writable by
  construction; decisions go on the submission row.
- **`Approve`/`Reject` must persist the `reviewer:` they are given**, and a
  new path that moves a product out of review must run the workflows or
  refuse — never write `status` around them.

## Open Questions

- Should a `proposed` product be editable by the seller, or frozen until the
  operator decides? Leaning editable, with an edit returning it to `draft`
  so the operator never reviews a moving target. Not blocking: the first cut
  leaves it editable and in place.
- Whether the operator needs a bulk approve for a seller's first batch.
  Part-answered (2026-08-26): `bulk_status_update` must NOT cover it — it
  writes around the review and records no reviewer, so it refuses products
  in review. A bulk approve, if wanted, is a loop over `Approve`.
- Whether `Spree::Sellers::Reject` moves onto the same shape. It stores its
  reason in `seller.metadata['rejection_reason']` identically; the erasure
  is proven for products and inferred (unverified) for sellers. A seller's
  review already has a natural home nearby (`SellerRequirementSubmission`
  carries `review_note` per requirement), so the fix there may be a column
  on the seller's review flow rather than a new model — decide when it is
  taken up.
