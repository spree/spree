---
title: "Seller Panel"
description: "Learn what a seller sees and can do in their own panel on your marketplace."
---

Every seller on your marketplace has a panel of their own. It is a separate sign-in from your admin dashboard, showing one seller their own products, orders and earnings, with no access to your dashboard and none to any other seller.

Every seller uses it at least once, because the panel is where they accept their invitation and work through your onboarding checklist. Nothing about that part is optional.

What happens after approval varies. A seller whose whole business runs on your marketplace works in the panel every day, listing products and fulfilling orders from it. A seller already trading on their own platform, such as a Shopify store connected to your marketplace, may never open it again, because their products and orders flow between the two systems and they manage both from the dashboard they already use. For that seller the panel was the onboarding step and nothing more.

Knowing what the panel holds is what lets you answer a seller who cannot find something, which is usually because the thing they are looking for belongs to your marketplace rather than to them.

## Home Screen

![The seller panel home screen](/docs/images/user/sellers/seller-panel/1-panel-home.png)

Home is where a seller lands after signing in. It opens with their business name and answers one question, which is where they stand with your marketplace.

Three figures sit across the top:

- **Status** - Their seller status, in the same words you see on their record. See [<u>Sellers</u>](/user/sellers/sellers).
- **Products** - How many products they have listed, counting drafts and products still in review.
- **Team members** - How many people can sign in and run the seller.

Below them, **Selling** states plainly whether their products are on sale or not yet.

A seller who has not finished onboarding also gets a **Finish setting up** card above all of this, showing how much of your checklist is done, with a **Continue setup** button into it. That card disappears once the checklist is complete rather than sitting at full for good.

<Note>**Selling** reports the seller's own standing rather than whether any shopper can actually buy from them. A seller reads that their products are on sale as soon as they are approved and not on holiday, even where none of their products has been listed on a sales channel. See [<u>Seller Products</u>](/user/sellers/products).</Note>

Home carries no sales figures, order counts or balance. Each of those lives on its own screen.

## What the Sidebar Holds

The sidebar is the whole panel. It is deliberately short compared to the admin dashboard.

| Screen | What the seller does there |
|---|---|
| **Home** | Reads their status, product count, team size and whether they are selling. |
| **Setup** | Works through your onboarding checklist and submits for review. |
| **Products** | Lists products, submits them for review, imports and exports. |
| **Orders** | Reads the orders they received and fulfills them. |
| **Earnings** | Reads what their sales earned, less your commission. |
| **Payouts** | Reads what has been paid to them, and what is on its way. |
| **Profile** | Writes their public profile and uploads their logo and cover photo. |
| **Settings** | Manages stock locations, delivery methods, package types, policies and their team. |

**Earnings** and **Payouts** are there because a seller needs to see their own money. Your dashboard has nothing like them for your own team, because the same question asked from your side covers every seller at once, which is what **Sellers → Seller transfers** and **Sellers → Payouts** answer.

## Setup

![The Setup checklist in the seller panel](/docs/images/user/sellers/seller-panel/2-setup-checklist.png)

**Setup** is your onboarding checklist as the seller sees it, with their progress at the top and each requirement as a row they expand to work on. **Submit for review** stays unavailable until everything you marked as required is done.

This screen is the one part of the panel every seller has to use. See [<u>Onboarding</u>](/user/sellers/onboarding).

## Products

A seller's product form is the same one your team uses, with the parts that belong to your marketplace left out. There is no sales channel control, no categories, collections or tags, and no tax category.

A seller cannot put a product on sale. They save it, submit it for review, and wait for your decision. See [<u>Seller Products</u>](/user/sellers/products).

## Orders

![A fulfilled order in the seller panel, with the carrier and tracking number](/docs/images/user/sellers/seller-panel/3-fulfilled-order.png)

A seller sees only the orders that are theirs. Where one shopper buys from two sellers in a single checkout, each seller sees their own part of it and neither sees the other's.

The order screen carries what has to be shipped, the customer's delivery address, and a summary showing what the order came to and the marketplace fee charged on it. The seller fulfills from here, entering a tracking number and choosing whether the customer is notified. Returns and claims are raised and handled on the same screen. See [<u>Marketplace Orders</u>](/user/sellers/orders).

## Earnings

![The Earnings screen in the seller panel](/docs/images/user/sellers/seller-panel/4-earnings.png)

**Earnings** is where a seller sees what their sales have made them. A balance card leads, carrying one position per currency, with **Owed to you** as the headline figure and three more beneath it:

- **Earned** - Everything their sales have earned, net of anything refunded.
- **Paid out** - How much of that has already reached them through a completed payout.
- **On its way** - Earnings a payment provider has not confirmed yet, so a seller who has shipped does not read their own order as unpaid.

Below the balance sits every earning line by line, with the date, the order it came from, whether it was a sale or a refund, its status and its amount.

The screen is read-only. An earning is written when an order is fulfilled and corrected by a refund when one is given, so there is nothing on it for a seller to change. The figures are the same ones you read under **Sellers → Seller transfers**. See [<u>Seller Transfers</u>](/user/sellers/transfers).

<Note>A seller never sees your commission rates. They see the fee charged on each of their own orders, which appears on the order itself, and the earning that fee was taken from.</Note>

## Payouts

![The Payouts screen in the seller panel](/docs/images/user/sellers/seller-panel/5-payouts.png)

**Payouts** is the settlements themselves, rather than the sales behind them. Each row carries the date, the status, how many earnings it covers, the reference and the amount.

The **Reference** is worth knowing about. Where you settle by bank transfer, it is the reference your team typed in when marking the payout as paid, which is what lets a seller match a payment against their own bank statement.

This screen is read-only as well. Payouts are created on the schedule you set and completed by your team or by the payment provider, so there is nothing here for a seller to act on. See [<u>Payouts</u>](/user/sellers/payouts).

## Profile and Settings

**Profile** holds the public description, the logo and the cover photo shoppers see, along with the seller's business details and tax registrations.

**Settings** holds what the seller runs their own fulfillment with, which is their stock locations, delivery methods and package types, plus their published policies and their team. See [<u>Onboarding</u>](/user/sellers/onboarding) for what each of those is for.

**Team** is where a seller invites colleagues by email. Everyone on a seller's team has the same access, so a colleague brought in to help with packing can also change prices, read earnings and remove other members, including the person who invited them.

<Warning>There are no roles within a seller team. Anyone a seller invites can do everything that seller can do.</Warning>
